Background

Ann Lucas was born in Illinois. She earned a bachelor's degree from the University of Phoenix in 2010.

As the incumbent representative for House District 43, I serve on the Corporations & Revenue Committees. During my time in office, I have sponsored or cosponsored 86 bills, 17 of which are now law. Before serving in the Legislature, I spent much of my adult life in retail banking, working in banks & credit unions. I worked my way up to executive management, retiring as Executive Vice President of a local credit union, with a strong background in personal finance, budgeting, lending, compliance, human resources, & financial analysis. I was a loan officer for many years & have experience in real estate, commercial, & consumer lending. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Wyoming’s biggest challenge is keeping government limited, transparent, and accountable to the people who pay the bills. State government must control spending, root out waste, and limit taxpayer dollars to necessary functions. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Wyoming currently spends almost a billion dollars annually on healthcare through Medicaid, CHIP, behavioral health, aging services, and other programs. The question is not simply whether government can spend more, but whether those dollars produce better access, lower costs, and better outcomes. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

I do not support backroom deals or blanket efforts to sell off Wyoming’s public lands. Government at all levels must be transparent in all land sales, transfers, exchanges, and management decisions. That means public notice, full disclosure of appraisals and terms, and input from affected citizens. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

Property tax relief must start with spending discipline. Counties are not all alike, but taxpayers are being asked to fund government growth faster than their income has grown. Paying rising property taxes over a lifetime is unsustainable. Laramie County is not poor. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The Legislature’s role in economic development must be limited, transparent, and focused on core infrastructure, not picking winners and losers. Government should provide a fair tax and regulatory climate, roads, water, power, broadband, and public safety, then let private businesses compete. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

Campaign donations must be transparent, lawful, and easy for citizens to review. I do not believe candidates should be controlled by PACs, lobbyists, or out-of-state interests. Voters deserve to know who is funding campaigns and whether elected officials represent citizens or donors. …
