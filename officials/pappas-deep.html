Research

ResidenceCheyenne
OccupationArchitect; president of Pappas & Pappas Architects, P.C.
Background

Born in Cheyenne, he practiced architecture for decades with his father, including school and university projects in the Cheyenne area.

He was elected to the Senate in 2014 and has chaired the Select Committee on School Facilities and, more recently, Transportation, Highways and Military Affairs, also serving on Revenue.

In March 2026 he announced he would not seek re-election. His current term ends January 2027.

EducationB.F.A., University of New Mexico, 1973; Bachelor of Architecture, Arizona State University, 1978; Cheyenne East High School, 1969
MilitaryWyoming Air National Guard, commonly cited 1985–2009, retiring as a brigadier general and assistant adjutant general
Prior officesWyoming Senate District 7, 2015–Present
Issue positions

2022 questionnaire priorities

In his 2022 candidate questionnaire he listed three priorities: a practical solution to structural fiscal deficits in education, health care, and transportation without “draconian cuts”; more stable funding sources to offset declining extractive-industry revenue; and determining the “right size” of government, then fully funding agreed services, including education, health care, roads and bridges, state employee compensation, retiree cost-of-living adjustments, and support for towns and counties.

2014 campaign themes

Earlier 2014 campaign themes included economic diversification, reduced regulatory burden, support for Common Core standards then in use, openness to a carefully designed Medicaid expansion because the state already paid for much uncompensated care, and a pro-life position that life begins at fertilization.

Medicaid expansion

He has consistently advocated Medicaid expansion and criticized the state’s rejection of large amounts of federal grant money, arguing the funds would otherwise go to other states and that uncompensated care already burdened the system.
