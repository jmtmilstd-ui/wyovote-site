Research

OccupationCattle rancher
Background

Charles K. “Charlie” Scott was born near Klamath Falls, Oregon; his family moved to Casper in 1948. As of 2026 he has more than 47 years of continuous legislative service.

He was prime sponsor of the 2005 Hathaway Scholarship, which he has repeatedly called his proudest accomplishment. He has long focused on K-12 education, health-care policy, property-tax relief, and state reserves against mineral-revenue volatility.

His current term runs through January 2029; the seat was not on the 2026 ballot.

EducationB.A., Harvard College, 1967; M.B.A., Harvard Business School, 1969
Prior officesWyoming House, 1979–1982; Wyoming Senate, 1983–Present (Senate District 30 since 1993); Senate vice president, 1993–1994
Issue positions

Education (August 2024 Oil City News Q&A)

He said K-12 is government’s top job, funding is generally adequate, and the problem is administration and reading instruction. He supported parental choice, charters, and incentives for districts to improve reading.

Property taxes

In the same 2024 Q&A he pledged a 25–50% cut on typical homes and noted he had already voted for a 25% reduction later vetoed by the governor.

Limited government and health care

He argued the state should do core functions well, stay skeptical of expansion, and keep building reserves because mineral revenues swing. He has opposed Medicaid expansion. He criticized local hospital-market problems and offered his committee experience to work on hospital pricing transparency and related fixes.
