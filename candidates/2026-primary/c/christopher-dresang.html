Background

Christopher Dresang was born in Casper, Wyoming. He earned a high school diploma from Natrona County High School, an associate degree from Casper College in 2002, a graduate degree from the University of Montana, Bozeman in 2012, and a Ph.D. degree from the University of Wyoming in 2026. Dresang's career experience includes working as a director in public school administration.

I'm a lifelong Natrona County resident, born and raised in Casper, and a proud University of Wyoming graduate. I've spent my career in education here at home, and my commitment to this community extends well beyond the workday. I've given my time to organizations that look out for our most vulnerable neighbors — serving on the Juvenile Justice Board, the Natrona County Child Protection Team, and the Self Help Center — and I've spent nearly a decade as secretary of the Wyoming Soccer Association. That service is rooted in a simple belief: the people closest to a problem are best equipped to solve it. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Wyoming's biggest challenge is keeping decisions in the hands of the people who live with the consequences. Too often, authority drifts away from local communities — like the recent school funding changes that cut roughly 8% from student activities and locked dollars into rigid categories, stripping local boards of …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Healthcare costs and access top voters' lists for good reason — too many Wyomingites drive hours for care, and rural communities are watching providers grow scarce. The Legislature can't fix everything, but it has real tools. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

No, I don't support selling off Wyoming's public lands. These lands belong to all of us: the hunters, anglers, ranchers, and families who've used them for generations. Once they're sold, they're gone — too often to the highest bidder from out of state, and the public loses access for good. That's not the Wyoming way. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

Wyoming homeowners deserve real relief — valuations climbed fast, and folks on fixed incomes got squeezed hard. I take that seriously. But here's what often gets lost: property tax is the most local source of revenue we have. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The Legislature's role isn't to pick winners and losers — it's to build the foundation that lets Wyoming businesses and communities thrive: a skilled workforce, reliable infrastructure, and strong institutions. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

My values here are simple: a campaign should be funded by the community it seeks to serve. I'm proud that in my last run, the vast majority of my individual donations came from right here in Natrona County, and my organizational support comes from Wyoming-based groups. …
