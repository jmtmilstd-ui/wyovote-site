Background

Ken Clouston was born in Bismarck, North Dakota, and lives in Wyoming. Clouston earned a B.S. in physical therapy in 1992 and a doctorate of physical therapy in 2005, both from the University of North Dakota. His career experience includes working as a physical therapist.

Ken is the founder and president of Gillette Physical Therapy, P.C., serving the Gillette community since 1993. A Doctor of PT and advocate for healthcare reform, he has focused on improving patient care, advancing standards, and expanding access. Since 2022, Ken has represented HD 32 in the WY Legislature, serving on the Education Committee and the Labor, Health, and Social Services Committee. He completed the Milbank Program for Emerging Leaders in Healthcare in 2025 and has participated in national policy efforts. He has led key healthcare legislation, including reforms to credentialing, prior authorization, reimbursement, and accessibility. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Wyoming’s biggest challenges are diversifying our economy, sustaining communities facing rising costs, and maintaining reliable public services in a rural state. At the same time, we have tremendous opportunities in energy innovation, healthcare delivery, and workforce development. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Healthcare affordability and access require reducing administrative burdens, increasing provider availability, and improving transparency. I’ve worked on reforms to prior authorization, credentialing, and reimbursement to lower costs and keep providers practicing in WY. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

Public lands are central to Wyoming’s economy, culture, and way of life. I do not support broad efforts to sell or transfer public lands out of public hands. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

Property tax relief is important, but it must be balanced carefully with the need to fund essential local services like schools, emergency response, and infrastructure. A one-size-fits-all solution doesn’t work in Wyoming. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The Legislature plays an important role in creating the conditions for economic growth, not picking winners and losers. Our job is to ensure a stable regulatory environment, invest in workforce development, and support infrastructure that enables businesses to succeed. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

Transparency and integrity must guide campaign finance. I support full disclosure so voters understand who supports candidates and why. Wyoming’s system works well in many respects, but there is room to improve clarity, accessibility of reporting, and public understanding. …
