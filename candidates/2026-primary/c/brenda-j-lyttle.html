Brenda J Lyttle

Brenda J Lyttle (Democratic, Cheyenne): Retired attorney (still practices); former administrator, Wyoming Department of Family Services programs; Laramie County Community College Board of Trustees (elected). Unopposed in the Aug. 18, 2026 Democratic primary for State House District 8.

House District 8 · Democratic

Primary result: Unopposed

General result: State House HD08, Democratic, 927 votes (22.9%), Lost.

Background

I am a retired attorney that still serves my community as a lawyer. I served as an administrator in the Child Support Enforcement, SNAP and TANF programs for the Wyoming Department of Family Services for several years. I most recently represented debtors in the National Debt Relief program. I'm active with St. Paul’s Lutheran Church in Cheyenne as the choir director and member of the Gifts Committee and Foundation Board. I served for 16 years as an elected Trustee to the Laramie County Community College Board of Trustees. I serve on the Board of Directors for My Front Door, a local housing program. I also act and direct for the community theatre and play saxophone for local band ensembles. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

I asked Wyoming voters on my Facebook page what they saw as the three top issues facing Wyoming's people. The top three responses were Protecting Public Education, Affordable and Accessible Healthcare, and Affordable and Accessible Housing. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

First, we need to explore a Wyoming solution to the federal cuts to the Affordable Care Act's enhanced premium tax cut. We also need to continue to look at Medicaid Expansion and the services Wyoming Medicaid will cover. I want to work with hospitals, doctors and insurers to discover solutions to these issues. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

I do not support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming. Our public lands are valuable to our health and culture. I met a young couple who said one reason they moved to Wyoming was enjoyment of our public lands. Tourism and outdoor recreation are the second-highest drivers of our economy. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

I oppose a statewide elimination or 50% reduction in property taxes. These cuts benefit the wealthy, high-value property owners in the state. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

I am a strong proponent of small business development. The new small businesses I see in Cheyenne are started by folks who are much younger than me and want to build a future in Wyoming. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

Every candidate must report campaign donations to the Wyoming Secretary of State. The Secretary and staff receive these reports and should take action if irregular or illegal activities occur. …
