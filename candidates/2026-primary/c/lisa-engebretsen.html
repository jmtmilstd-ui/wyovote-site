Lisa Engebretsen

Lisa Engebretsen (Republican, Casper): Real Estate Broker. Won the Aug. 18, 2026 Republican primary for State Senate District 29 with 52.4% of the vote.

Senate District 29 · Republican

Primary result: Won

General result: State Senate SD29, Republican, 4,531 votes (100%), Unopposed.

Research

ResidenceCasper, Natrona County
OccupationReal Estate Broker
Background

Engebretsen graduated from Kelly Walsh High School. Engebretsen earned an associate degree from Casper College in 1988. Engebretsen's career experience includes working as a real estate broker.

I own my business Forefront Real Estate and have been a business owner for 10 years. I have worked in various industries - oilfield, trucking, newspaper, and real estate. I served on Casper City Council for 4 years and was Vice Mayor for a 1 1/2 years. I am currently the Vice President of Wyoming Realtors, I served on Natrona County Historic Preservation Board, and I serve on Central Wyoming advocacy board for Central Wyoming Realtors. I love our great state and want to preserve our values, support economic growth, and ensure future generations can enjoy the freedoms we cherish today.

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Wyoming's biggest challenge is protecting our way of life while preparing for the future. We are facing rising property taxes, housing shortages, rural healthcare gaps, education concerns, workforce shortages, and an economy that still depends heavily on energy and mineral revenue. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Healthcare affordability and access are among the most important issues facing Wyoming families, especially in our rural communities. No Wyoming resident should have to drive hours to see a specialist, wait months for care, or choose between paying for medication and paying their bills. As a legislator. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

I believe public lands are on of Wyoming's greatest assets and should be managed in a way that benefits the people of Wyoming. I do not support large scale sales or transfers of public lands if they would reduce public access, harm Wyoming's outdoor heritage, or place additional financial burdens on taxpayers. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

Property tax relief is important because many Wyoming families, seniors, and small business owners are feeling the pressure of rising property values and rising tax bills. People should not be taxed out of their homes simply because the market value of their property has increased. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The Wyoming Legislature has an important role in economic development but is not government's job to pick winners and losers in the marketplace. Our role should be to create an environment where businesses, entrepreneurs, and workers can succeed. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

My approach to campaign donations is simple: transparency, integrity, and accountability. Voters deserve to know who is supporting candidates and where campaign money is coming from. Public trust in government depends on an open and transparent process.
