Larry Alwin

Larry Alwin (Democratic, Thermopolis): Independent business-intelligence consultant, working from home. Earlier taught computer classes to adults for about three years, then spent about a decade at a Colorado company that made software for school districts, becoming its director of analytics. He said much of the consulting work had dried up over the past year as artificial intelligence took hold. Lost the Nov. 3, 2026 general election for State Representative H28 with 13.2% of the vote.

General result: State House HD28, Democratic, 504 votes (13.2%), Lost.

Background

Thermopolis resident (mailing city on the candidate filing) running as a Democrat for House District 28.

Grew up in a ranching family in western Nebraska; he told a 2024 forum he was born in Harris, Nebraska, the middle of five children, just across the Wyoming border on Highway 20. After graduating from Chadron State with a teaching degree he taught computer classes to adults, then moved into software work. He later moved to Thermopolis for his husband’s job.

Also ran for House District 28 in 2024 as a Democrat, after receiving enough write-in votes in the primary to accept the nomination, and lost to incumbent Republican John Winter. He told the Cody Enterprise he received about 20% of the vote in that race.

Issue positions

Why he is running

Said people deserve a choice on the ballot and that he was running even though he expected to lose. It was his second campaign for House District 28.

Taxes

Opposes Proposition 1, saying it would strain county budgets; he said the last round of property-tax cuts forced his county to cut its budget by $2.2 million, reducing funding for programs such as 4-H and the library. Instead he wants tax relief aimed at retirees, including exempting retirees from property taxes.

Education and public lands

Opposes Wyoming’s school voucher program and called education “the most important thing in Wyoming.” Opposes selling public lands to private owners: “Keep our public lands in public hands.”

Rural infrastructure

Wants more investment in rural infrastructure such as cell service, saying there is no complete cell service between Thermopolis and Powell.

Health care (2024 forum)

At an October 2024 Chamber of Commerce forum he supported freedom of choice in health care and expanding Medicaid, and said government should stay out of “bedrooms and our doctor’s offices” while regulating industries that harm people.
