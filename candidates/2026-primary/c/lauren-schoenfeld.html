Background

Lauren Schoenfeld earned a high school diploma from Rock Springs High School, an associate degree from Central Wyoming College, and a bachelor's degree from the University of Wyoming.

My Wyoming roots run deep. Raised in Farson, I have an appreciation for hard work, independence, and service. My experience in business, community engagement, and policy shaped my belief that our future should be built by the people who live, work, and raise families here. I am proud of the Wyoming way of life and I believe our families want safe communities, strong economies, protected freedoms, and conservative leaders who will fight for their values. I am running for Wyoming State Senate to bring principled, conservative leadership. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Wyoming shaped me. Growing up in here, I learned hard work, self-reliance, and what it means to be part of a community. That was my Wyoming — and I want it to still be Wyoming when my daughter grows up. Keeping Wyoming strong for our youth means protecting their constitutional freedoms. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Wyoming is a big state with small towns, and healthcare access is a real challenge. Here is how I would address it. Expand telehealth so rural families can access quality care without driving hours to see a provider. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

I do not. Wyoming's public lands are part of who we are — where we hunt, fish, ranch, and make our living. Keeping them public means every Wyoming family has access to the land that defines this state. But keeping land public is not enough. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

Wyoming's tax structure needs more than quick fixes — it needs a long-term, comprehensive review that looks at the full picture. I support a measured approach that does two things at once: delivers real relief to Wyoming families and businesses, and ensures our state can still fund the services people depend on. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The legislature's role in economic development is to create conditions where Wyoming businesses can grow and thrive. That means smart infrastructure investment, a predictable regulatory environment, and support that empowers communities. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

Campaign donations are an investment in the democratic process — nothing more, nothing less. When someone donates to my campaign, they are helping fund the practical work of running for office: yard signs, mailers, events, and the tools needed to reach voters across Senate District 5. That is what donations do. …
