Rah Reinholz

Rah Reinholz (Democratic, Rock Springs): Laborer. Unopposed in the Aug. 18, 2026 Democratic primary for State House District 17.

House District 17 · Democratic

Primary result: Unopposed

General result: State House HD17, Democratic, 692 votes (32.7%), Lost.

Research

ResidenceRock Springs, Sweetwater County
OccupationLaborer
Background

Rah Reinholz attended the Western Wyoming Community College. Reinholz's career experience includes working as a laborer.

I’m a working-class Wyomingite, blue collar manual labor. I’ve spent years showing up for democracy as an election judge spending nights counting your ballots, traveling to other cities to be a delegate for Wyoming Democrats, phone banking, door knocking, and calling state and local officials who answered me with copy and paste form that amounted little more then silence. I follow the news, pay my bills, and still believe the process belongs to us. I've grown tired of watching decisions made about us, I've realized the people who live the laws should help write them. I’m not a career politician, just a Wyomingite who’s tired of seeing our voices left outside the Capitol doors. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

The biggest and broadest issue facing Wyoming as a whole is our dependence on the boom busted revenue. If mineral prices crash, severance tax vanishes and we slash schools, healthcare, and childcare leaving a barren system for growing families and an economic environment that can't support our graduates. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Healthcare costs are suffocating because our boom bust budget leaves hospitals under funded and workers under paid. Wyoming is one of ten states still refusing a corporate income tax, so we can’t build clinics, forgive rural nurse loans, or expand Medicaid wrap around funds that keep small hospitals solvent. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

As a legislator I support public lands remaining in the public domain; they belong to every Wyomingite, and once they’re gone, they’re gone for good. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

Property tax relief sounds great until small towns can’t keep their lights on and high schools are talking about cutting sports. I won’t support blanket cuts or hikes that slam fixed income neighbors. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The Wyoming Legislature should absolutely act as a facilitator in economic development. We must diversify beyond volatile severance taxes by developing knowledge intensive sectors. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

I value elections where ideas beat bank accounts, not the biggest wallet. Wyoming should cap campaign spending based on registered voter counts for legislative races and set firm limits for statewide offices. Require every PAC or nonprofit to disclose the real, living donors, no more shell game front groups. …
