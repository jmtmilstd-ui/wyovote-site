Background

Ivan Posey served in the United States Army. His career experience includes working for the USDA Forest Service in the Shoshone National Forest and as culture director for Arapahoe School in the Fremont County School District #38. Posey was appointed by Gov. Dave Freudenthal (D) to serve as the first Tribal Liaison for the Shoshone and Arapaho Tribes of the Wind River Indian Reservation from 2003 to 2004. He is an enrolled member of the Eastern Shoshone tribe and is also Northern Cheyenne and Northern Arapaho. Posey has been affiliated with Americans for Indian Opportunity, Advancement for Indigenous Opportunity International, the Wind River Development Fund, and Community Entry Services. …

Attended school at Fort Washakie Elementary and Tribal Boarding School in Chilocco, Oklahoma. Served in the US Army and started my career in Natural Resources in 1983. Worked for the Bureau of Indian Affairs and Shoshone National Forest from 1983-1994 and was elected to the Shoshone Business Council in 1994. Served the Eastern Shoshone Tribe for 21 years before coming to Central Wyoming College in 2016 where I serve as the Coordinator for Tribal Education. Attended school at Central Wyoming College. I have three children and two grandsons.

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Obviously there are several challenges throughout the state, from rising costs and access to healthcare, education and the exodus of our graduates out of the state, Public lands access and continued care for our seniors.

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Medicaid expansion, access to specialized treatments locally, expand mental health services.

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

I do not support. Some efforts have to be made at the federal level such as funding much needed positions in natural resource agencies.

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

We have to be very aware of the funding issues with smaller municipalities and the drastic effects it will have on those communities. One size don't fit all.

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

It should have some role but not the major role. Educating communities on the pathway to economic possibilities for individuals is important and assisting in larger projects through transparency and partnerships.

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

The campaign system in the nation needs reformed.
