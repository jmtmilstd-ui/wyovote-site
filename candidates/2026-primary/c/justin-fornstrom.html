Background

Justin Fornstrom was born in Falls Church, Virginia. He earned a bachelor's degree in health sciences from the University of Wyoming. Fornstrom's career experience includes working as a farmer and a business owner. He has also served as the Laramie County Fire District 5 president and the mayor of Pine Bluffs, Wyoming.

I am a fourth-generation farmer and small business owner, representing the people of House District 10. Raised in Pine Bluffs, I graduated from Pine Bluffs High School in 1990 and earned a bachelor's degree from the University of Wyoming in 1995. After college, I returned home to farm with my brother and help grow several local businesses. My commitment to service includes 16 years as President of Laramie County Fire District 5, election as Mayor of Pine Bluffs in 2021, and appointment to the Wyoming House of Representatives in 2025. I also serve on the Board of Directors for the Wyoming Municipal Power Agency and represent Eastern Laramie County through Cheyenne LEADS. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Wyoming’s biggest challenges are affordability, healthcare access, workforce shortages, housing, infrastructure and protecting our core industries such as energy and agriculture. Our opportunities include: energy, agriculture, tourism, small business, outdoor recreation and a strong quality of life. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Healthcare is especially difficult in a rural state with long distances and small populations. Wyoming should support rural hospitals and clinics, expand telehealth, grow our healthcare workforce, reduce unnecessary regulation and look for ways to increase competition and transparency in pricing. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

I generally oppose the sale of public lands. Public lands are an important part of Wyoming's heritage and support recreation, grazing, hunting, tourism, and economic development. Once public lands are sold, they are often lost to public access permanently. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

People need property tax relief, especially homeowners on fixed incomes, but counties, schools, fire districts and local governments also rely on that revenue. I support targeted, responsible relief that protects residents without creating budget crises for essential services. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The Legislature should not pick winners and losers, but it should create the conditions for growth: low taxes, reasonable regulation, reliable infrastructure, workforce training, housing availability and support for Wyoming’s core industries. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

My values are simple: be transparent, follow the law and never let a donation influence a vote. People deserve to know who is supporting candidates and how money is being spent. …
