Melissa Butcher

Melissa Butcher (Republican, Ranchester): Public process facilitator (30+ years); former executive director, Sheridan County Chamber of Commerce; former WYDOT public involvement specialist. Won the Aug. 18, 2026 Republican primary for State Senate District 21 with 58.4% of the vote.

Background

I attended Casper College, the University of Oregon and Trend Business College. My professional background includes service in both the public and private sectors. After college I worked as a legal assistant in Washington State. Since returning to Wyoming in 1995 I have worked as the administrative services manager for the Central Wyoming Regional Water System, a public involvement specialist for the Wyoming Department of Transportation, executive director of the Sheridan County Chamber of Commerce, and for more than 30 years as a public process facilitator helping communities navigate complex issues and long-range planning. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Wyoming's biggest challenge is keeping the promise of Wyoming within reach for the next generation. Families are facing rising property taxes, healthcare costs, housing pressures, workforce shortages, and concerns about long-term economic stability. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Healthcare affordability and access are major concerns across Wyoming, especially in rural communities where provider shortages and long travel distances can make care difficult to obtain. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

I do not support broad efforts to sell or transfer Wyoming's public lands. Public lands are an important part of our economy, outdoor heritage, and way of life. They support grazing, hunting, fishing, recreation, wildlife habitat, and responsible energy development. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

Property tax relief is necessary. Many Wyoming homeowners, especially seniors and families on fixed incomes, have seen tax bills rise faster than their ability to pay. People should not be taxed out of homes they worked hard to own. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

Economic development is not about government creating prosperity. It is about creating the conditions that allow people, businesses, and communities to succeed. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

Campaign donations should never buy influence. My responsibility is to the people of Senate District 21, not to any donor or political organization. I believe voters deserve transparency about who is funding campaigns and political advertising, especially when outside groups are involved. …
