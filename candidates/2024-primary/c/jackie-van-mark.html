Jackie Van Mark

Profile · 2024 Primary

Jackie Van Mark (Republican, Torrington). Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 5 with 36.4% of the vote.

Primary result: Lost, 891 votes (36.4%), 2nd of 2.

Research

ResidenceTorrington, Goshen County
OccupationManages the family dryland wheat and sunflower farm; small real-estate business in Torrington (2024 WyoFile questionnaire)
Background

In her 2024 WyoFile questionnaire, Jackie Van Mark wrote that she is a fourth-generation Goshen County dryland wheat farmer, was born and raised in Torrington on the family farm, has lived in Goshen County her entire life, and would be 55 on Election Day 2024. She wrote that she manages the family farm and has a small business selling real estate, after earlier work in Washington, D.C., for U.S. Sen. Craig Thomas and President George W. Bush, and work for the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs.

She wrote that she is chair of the Board of Trustees for the Eastern Wyoming Community College District, a graduate of Wyoming Ag Leadership Class VI and of Bethel College (University) in St. Paul, Minnesota, a past member of the Goshen County Nursing Home Joint Powers Board and the Goshen County Community Hospital Foundation, and an alumna of Valley Christian School. She listed endorsements from Americans for Prosperity, Wyoming Right to Life, and Wyo Realtors.

Issue positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Civility. The Wyoming legislature has long been an example of civility to other states. The past two sessions have seen that begin to erode. Governing is about building relationships and getting things done. Fighting accomplishes nothing. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

By working with local officials and knowing and understanding the needs of the community. As a legislator it is my job to remove the obstacles that hinder local government from doing their job.

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

As a Republican I firmly believe that our strength as a state is our people. If we keep our legacy industries, Agriculture, Coal, Oil, Gas, Trona, healthy our people can have good jobs and our state does well. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

Compromise is not a bad word. Anyone in a relationship has to compromise if they want that relationship to continue. Politics and Governing is no different. I will focus on getting the facts and also understanding the perspective of my colleagues and my constituents. …

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

Private property rights are extremely important. This is a situation where compromise and perspective are also key to finding the right mix. With out wildlife and open spaces we loose who we are as a state and we loose the value to our land. …
