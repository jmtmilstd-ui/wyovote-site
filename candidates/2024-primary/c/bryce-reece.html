Bryce Reece

Bryce Reece (Republican, Casper): Small-business owner (since 2019); previously executive vice president of a livestock trade organization (20 years, including lobbying). Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State Senate District 28 with 49.4% of the vote.

Republican · Casper, Natrona County

Primary result: Lost, 1,274 votes (49.4%), 2nd of 2.

Research

OccupationSmall-business owner (since 2019); previously executive vice president of a livestock trade organization (20 years, including lobbying)
Background

My wife Melodie and I have owned a small business since 2019. In a previous life, I spent 20 years as the Executive Vice President of a livestock trade organization. As part of that position, I lobbied, so I have 20 years of legislative/lobbying experience. I have served on boards and commissions in the past, including four years on the State Fair Advisory Board, with two of those as Chairman. I served as a member of the University of Wyoming College of Agriculture Advisory Board for 20 years. I was also appointed by the USDA Secretary of Agriculture to two terms on the National Wildlife Damage Advisory Committee. I am a graduate of the Wyoming L.E.A.D. program, serving in L.E.A.D. Class II. In college, I was a Senator in the University of Wyoming Student Senate and I was a State FFA Officer. A good leader has the ability to connect and communicate with those by whom he has been placed in that position of leadership. They should and must be his priority in representing them. An exceptional leader must be dedicated, passionate, fearless, compassionate, caring and humble, and he must always strive to put the needs of his constituency ahead of his own. He has a responsibility to make sure he is educated on the issues in order to be an effective and productive advocate for those who elected him. Finally, a leader has a responsibility to also be a teacher and mentor by striving to make sure those coming after him have the opportunity to learn and excel so that the next generation of leaders will be ready to continue moving forward.

Positions

What committee assignments would you be most interested in and why?

I have interest in the Revenue Committee, the Corporations Committee, the Transportation Committee, the Minerals Committee, and the Agriculture Committee.

How would you prioritize capital spending/infrastructure in the state?

Our road system is my number-one priority in terms of infrastructure. Several of our economic sectors (tourism, manufacturing, agriculture, oil and gas) are wholly or heavily dependent upon a functioning, safe and efficient road system. We have failed to adequately maintain or improve our roads for a number of years, to the point that the cost to repair and improve our road system across the state is in the hundreds of billions of dollars. I do not agree with the emphasis that the current state leadership has apparently placed on replacing or constructing new buildings around the state while ignoring our roads. I am also in support of increasing our spending on water projects around the state. Water is the lifeblood of Wyoming and we have an aging water infrastructure system that needs a large infusion of capital in order to repair, restore and enhance it.

What is a state government issue that you think is overlooked?

Even though it has garnered the most discussion and attention this election season, the issue of property taxes and property tax relief has been overlooked and failed upon by the Legislature and the Governor. I fully expected the legislature to address and fix the property tax issue this past session, and while the conservatives in the Legislature tried, they were thwarted by the establishment Legislators (such as my opponent) and the governor, who appear to be content in keeping people in continual fear of not being able to pay their property taxes and losing their homes. That issue appears to have come home to roost on many of them and I think several will lose their legislative seats due to their inaction on this issue. Another local issue that was overlooked was how bad the state land office and the state land board totally dropped the ball on the proposal for the gravel pit on Coates Road. If the state land office and the governor had been doing their due diligence on this issue, they could have saved putting our community, and the proposed developer, through the turmoil and agony that has transpired. A proposal as involved and contentious as this should have never been relegated onto a ”consent list” for approval by the State Land Board. This is just another example of the complete disconnect there is between state government in Cheyenne and the rest of the state.

What can the Legislature do to improve public health?

There are several things that the Legislature can, and should, do to improve public health. The first is to put safeguards in place to assure or citizens that never again can our Governor force illogical and unscientific mandates down on us in the name of a politically contrived “public health emergency.” The second is to make sure that people will always have access to their own doctors and that entities such as Banner Health CANNOT prohibit individual doctors from communicating with their patients, such as was done during the “scamdemic.” Third is to assure our citizens that they will always have the ability to make their own health decisions and not have those decision dictated by the government, as was done during the “scamdemic.” This includes prohibiting employers from requiring untested and unapproved vaccines as a condition of employment. Fourth is to make sure our schools are sticking to educating our children and they are not indoctrinating them into woke ideologies that serve to confuse them and increase mental stresses on them. One other area that needs to be investigated is the area of medical malpractice insurance and the exorbitantly high costs that doctors incur in order to practice medicine and whether or not there is something the state can do to cause those costs to be reduced.
