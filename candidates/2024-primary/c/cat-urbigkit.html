Cat Urbigkit

Profile · 2024 Primary

Cat Urbigkit (Republican, Pinedale). Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 20 with 37.0% of the vote.

Primary result: Lost, 1,029 votes (37%), 2nd of 2.

Research

ResidenceBig Sandy area, Sublette County (Pinedale mailing city on the candidate filing)
OccupationWriter and journalist; she and her husband run livestock in the Big Sandy area (2024 WyoFile questionnaire)
Background

In her 2024 WyoFile Election Guide questionnaire, Cat Urbigkit wrote that she lives and ranches in the Big Sandy area of Sublette County, that she has spent most of her life in the county after moving there with her family from a small town in southern Indiana when she was 12, and that she would be 59 on Nov. 5, 2024.

The same questionnaire said she graduated from Pinedale High School, married her husband Jim at 20, and that after their son graduated from Big Piney High School they moved to the Big Sandy area, where they run livestock. She described herself as a reader and writer with work in articles, opinion columns, books, news media, and scientific journals; she started in journalism at the Pinedale Roundup, was one of the founders of the Sublette Examiner, later left newspaper work to write nonfiction books, owned a sheep-industry magazine for 12 years before selling it, and continued to write for a variety of publications. She also wrote that she has a history of holding public officials accountable and of researching and reporting on government programs.

Issue positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Our challenges and opportunities are intertwined. We need to support our legacy industries while diversifying our economy. We should remain a top energy producer through development of our rich variety of energy resources. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

The Wyoming Constitution provides for state obligations in certain areas (uniformity in taxation, providing system of public instruction, etc.). …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

The purpose of government is to support the well-being of its citizens and protect our individual rights. Our constitution repeatedly calls for actions for “public good and general welfare” and support of the poor. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

Compromise is cast as negative when it’s structured as giving up something to the opposition – but that’s not how I see most legislative compromises. …

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

I support multiple use and thoughtful site-specific solutions generated by stakeholders instead of blanket prohibitions. Only 20% of Sublette County is private land, and 1 in every 5 acres of that is protected from development through conservation easements. …
