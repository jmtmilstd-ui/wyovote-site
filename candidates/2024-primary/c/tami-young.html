Tami Young

Tami Young (Republican, Worland): Co-owner, with her husband, of Young’s Camper Repair in Worland (launched 2020); also described on her campaign site as working part-time as an orthodontist assistant and as an Emotion Code, Body Code, and Belief Code practitioner. Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 27 with 37.0% of the vote.

Republican · Worland, Washakie County

Primary result: Lost, 885 votes (37%), 2nd of 2.

Research

OccupationCo-owner, with her husband, of Young’s Camper Repair in Worland (launched 2020); also described on her campaign site as working part-time as an orthodontist assistant and as an Emotion Code, Body Code, and Belief Code practitioner
Background

Describes six-generation Wyoming roots to early settlers of Crystal Creek north of Greybull. Grew up in Worland, later graduated from Kelly Walsh High School after her family moved to Casper, and returned to Worland in 2009 after a 2009 divorce, later marrying her husband, Tim; together they have eight children. Her campaign biography also says she previously taught English grammar at Provo High School through Utah Valley University, worked as a photographer, is fluent in Spanish, and has served in the Washakie County GOP as precinct committee member, state committeewoman, and chairman, with more than 13 years in local Republican Party work.

EducationGraduated from Kelly Walsh High School in Casper. Associate’s degree in photography; bachelor’s degree from Brigham Young University with a focus on marriage and family therapy (her campaign site called it a Bachelor of Science; a 2024 Basin Republican Rustler candidate profile said a bachelor’s “with an emphasis in social services and marriage and family therapy”). Between the two degrees she served a year-and-a-half church mission in Argentina.
Positions

Core values

In a candidate-submitted Rustler profile she said her core values are “freedom, life, the Second Amendment, family, minimal government spending, low taxes, and small government,” and that taxes and spending “should never be more than necessary to meet the government’s constitutional obligations.” She described herself as pro-freedom, pro-life, pro-Second Amendment, pro-family, and an advocate for fiscal responsibility, and a staunch supporter of the Republican Party platform.

Budget, schools and economy

Wrote that Wyoming did not face a budget-funding crisis but “over-taxation and excessive spending,” citing roughly $30 billion in reserves. She argued other Western states were unconstitutionally restricting shipment of Wyoming coal and that the state should reduce regulatory burdens, lower taxes, and protect property rights. On school finance she said she did not believe Wyoming had a funding crisis and that the state should focus on wiser spending and better allocation rather than cuts or new revenue.

Role of government

Said a legislator’s role is to protect rights and liberties, defend the Constitution, and keep government limited and accountable, including life, property rights, free speech, and the right to bear arms, and to defend state sovereignty against federal overreach. She said she supported 2023 House Bill 212 to create standing committees reviewing the constitutionality of federal actions.
