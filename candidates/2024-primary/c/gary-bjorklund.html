Gary Bjorklund

Gary Bjorklund (Republican, Wheatland): Airline Pilot; U.S. Air Force, 1971–1976. Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State Senate District 6 with 5.6% of the vote.

Republican · Wheatland, Platte County

Primary result: Lost, 264 votes (5.6%), 6th of 6.

Research

OccupationAirline Pilot
Background

Gary Bjorklund was born in Billings, Montana. He served in the U.S. Air Force from 1971 to 1976. He graduated from Hot Springs County High School. He earned a bachelor's degree from the University of Wyoming in 1971. His career experience includes working as an airline pilot and in hardware retail. Bjorklund has been affiliated with the following organizations: NRA Experimental Aircraft Association Heritage Foundation Judicial Watch

Graduated from UW, became a pilot with USAF. Flew for United Airlines for 30 years. While on furlough from UAL went on active duty for the Navy. My wife and I opened a hardware store in Douglas in 1989 with my parents. My wife and I opened an ACE Hardware in Wheatland in 2002 and in 2013 we moved to Wheatland to operate the store full time. Sold the business last year and can no longer sit in the sidelines and watch what is happenong to our country and state. I am a very conservative guy.

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Taxes - There was a citizen petition with great possibilities that we didn't get enough signatures on last year. There was a bill in the legislature last year that wound have rolled the home valuations back to pre covid values and limited increases to 3%. We need to try again. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

Less government is better government. However you can't have totally different rules in each county. You can't have RPG's ok in Platte, AR-15's illegal in Laramie and normal capacity magazines banned in Albany. By the way my view on gun control is you practice enough to hit where you aim.

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

Economic development is the tide that raises all ships. People leave all small communities to shop in a larger community. With them go the tax dollars to support much of where they live. In some cases they say they can't find it at home but they didn't look. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

I'm willing to listen (convince me of the benefits to the citizens not government or big corporations) but over time conservatives have given a lot to get nothing. Which is why we are in the situation we are today.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

Every situation is different and I don't think you can paint it with a broad brush. I think we have enough more than enough wilderness areas. Open spaces for multiple uses are a good thing. I think we need to give all wildlife management back to the Wyoming Game and Fish.
