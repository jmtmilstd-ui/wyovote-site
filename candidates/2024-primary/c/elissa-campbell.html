Elissa Campbell

Elissa Campbell (Republican, Casper): Executive Director; Wyoming House District 56, 2025–Present. Won the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 56 with 55.3% of the vote. Unopposed in the Nov. 5, 2024 general election.

Republican · Casper, Natrona County

Primary result: Won, 806 votes (55.3%), 1st of 3.

General result: State House HD56, Republican, 2,938 votes (100%), Unopposed.

OccupationExecutive Director
Background

Elissa Campbell earned an associate degree from Casper College in 1993, and bachelor's degree in philosophy and environmental ethics from the University of Wyoming in 1994. Her career experience includes working as a business owner, executive director, and in training and development. Campbell has been affiliated with the Rotary, the Casper Committee on Foreign Relations, and the Casper Area Chamber of Commerce.

I’m a lifelong Wyoming Republican, who believes in local control, fiscal responsibility, solving problems, principles of good governance, and doing right by those who call Wyoming home. I am a small business owner in Casper, working in the training & development industry for over 25 years, serving every industry in Wyoming from oil & gas to local municipalities; finance to manufacturing; law enforcement to healthcare. In 2021, I was appointed to the Governor’s Wyoming Wildlife Task Force (WWTF) and was fortunate to work on top-priority wildlife policy issues with State Representatives, Senators, County Commissioners, and our Wyoming Game & Fish Department. …

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Serving on the Governor's WWTF, I learned the importance of understanding issues from a variety of perspectives, leaning on experts to help me understand, and the critical importance of engaging constituents and those impacted by decisions made. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

Whether we are considering control between the Federal government and the State of Wyoming or control between the State of Wyoming and Wyoming Counties/Municipalities, I think it's critical that those most impacted by the decision are engaged in the decision-making process. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

Constitutionally, the State is obligated to provide essential services that provide for/support the overall well being and quality of life for Wyoming residents. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

I believe good governance requires us to understand issues from a variety of perspectives, engage in civil discourse, and work together toward solutions that are meaningful and beneficial to Wyoming.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

Having served on the Governor’s WWTF, I spent 18-months working with State Representatives, Senators, County Commissioners, Land Owners, Outfitters, Hunters and Anglers, and our Wyoming Game & Fish Department. We were tasked with finding that balance on top-priority wildlife policy issues. …
