Background

In his 2024 WyoFile questionnaire, Phil Christopherson wrote that he was born and raised in Casper, worked in his family’s trucking business, graduated from Natrona County High School, and earned a degree in electrical engineering from the University of Wyoming. He wrote that he would be 64 on Election Day 2024, married Lori Hauck in 1982, and that they have five children and 12 grandchildren. After UW he worked at Boeing in the Seattle area on AWACS avionics and later as a project manager; he also wrote that he served as a reserve police officer for the King County Police, helped form the city of Covington, and was elected to its first city council. He and his wife moved back to Wyoming in 1999.

He wrote that after returning he started a small computer-service business, taught lean manufacturing around the state for the University of Wyoming Manufacturing Extension Partnership, led IDEA Inc., Riverton’s economic-development group, and earned an M.B.A. from UW in 2006. The family moved to Gillette in 2014, where he was CEO of Energy Capital Economic Development; he wrote that in that role he helped start a business incubator and the Wyoming Innovation Center.

Issue positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Facing challenges in the traditional minerals industry like coal, oil, and natural gas, we must support these vital sectors with innovative approaches to harness our resources and ensure affordable energy. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

I support local government. They are closest to the citizens and can best answer issues for their community. Local issues should be decided at the local level. Phil reaffirms his commitment to preserving Wyoming as a great place to live. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

These are all vital issues and the state does have a role to play. The primary role of state government is to support the local communities in solving their issues at they see fit. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

Our nation was founded on the idea of compromise and legislators must be willing to work together for the betterment of all. Compromising our ideas, plans and such is a good thing, while compromising our values is not.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

Multiple uses of public areas should be supported. Decisions and directions must be studied and understood prior to making long term decisions.
