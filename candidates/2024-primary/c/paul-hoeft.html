Paul Hoeft

Paul Hoeft (Republican, Powell): Mechanic; Wyoming House District 25, 2025–Present. Won the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 25 with 60.8% of the vote. Unopposed in the Nov. 5, 2024 general election.

Republican · Powell, Park County

Primary result: Won, 1,251 votes (60.8%), 1st of 2.

General result: State House HD25, Republican, 3,673 votes (100%), Unopposed.

Background

Paul Hoeft was born in Oak Harbor, Washington. He earned a high school diploma from Burlington-Edison High School. He earned an associate degree from the Bellingham Technical Institute in 1978. His career experience includes working as a mechanic.

Retired General Motors Service Technician and Service Manager of 40 years. Current Sporting Goods department Manager in retail store. Church Elder. 25+ year volunteer NRA Foundation. Executive Board member of local Gun Club. Instructor of Automotive technology. Experienced in budgets and cost analysis.

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Many of today's challenges are best resolved by the citizens of the State. Too many times legislatures overreach hinder the resolution of today's problems. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

By the guidelines of the State Constitution. Those powers not specifically given to the State Legislature, go to local control.

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

The well-being of a citizen is a difficult thing to define. To decide this definition with the use of public funds is troubling. The aforementioned issues should be made by decision of the individual and a free market system. Governmental insertion rarely leads to resolution.

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

As long as the compromise is within the guidelines of the State Constitution and the law.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

It is often difficult to balance many of these issues. Modern technology has brought us the ability for energy, industry and wildlife to operate in the same spaces. Real estate development is more difficult as these can cause a direct negative impact on wildlife and open spaces. …
