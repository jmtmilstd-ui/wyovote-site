Tamara Trujillo

Profile · 2024 Primary

Tamara Trujillo (Republican, Cheyenne): Administrator and auditor in the private sector; worked 17 years for a large oil company prominent in Wyoming, including policy writing, process improvement, and contracts (self-described, 2026); Wyoming House District 44, 2023–2024; Wyoming House of Representatives District 44, 2023–2025. Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 44 with 33.0% of the vote.

Primary result: Lost, 190 votes (33%), 2nd of 3.

OccupationAdministrator and auditor in the private sector; worked 17 years for a large oil company prominent in Wyoming, including policy writing, process improvement, and contracts (self-described, 2026)
Background

I am the current Representative for HD44. My experience I bring is the 15 years in the oil industry where I work in the accounting and contracts departments. I bring my ability to negotiate and work with different array of people. As for who I am, I am a single parent to grown children and I'm a grandma to 4 granddaughters.

In a 2026 campaign interview she said she had been an administrator and auditor in private work, including 17 years with a large oil company in Wyoming.

Prior officesWyoming House District 44, 2023–2024; Wyoming House of Representatives District 44, 2023–2025
Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Tax reform is a big deal to everyone, so to fix that we need to reform government spend. If we cut back spending for a few years we can change our tax structure without sacrificing our schools and the Wyoming people's pockets. In the process we need to bring jobs that employee and invest in the Wyoming working class.

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

It's important when making decisions on the State level to communicate with local officials such as city, county, school boards and other important agency's and church leaders who are closest to the people. It's important to get involve in the community to understand the needs of everyone.

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

We need to bring more affordable housing to Wyoming, this would then bring more jobs and the economy would thrive. We currently have billionaires coming to Wyoming that don't employee but a handful of people. We can bring companies that support those industries and bring more jobs to Wyoming. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

I'm willing to look at everything openly as I have with understanding the role of government and self accountability. Healthy debate and difference of opinion brings a compromise for all.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

Wildlife and open spaces shouldn't have to be a versus anything. Wyoming is supplying other states the energy as those state impose their own regulations on how we conduct business, this in turn cost the people wit higher rates. We can grow our energy resource at a responsible rate with new and innovated ideas. …
