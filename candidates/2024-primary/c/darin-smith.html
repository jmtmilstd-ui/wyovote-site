Darin Smith

Darin Smith (Republican, Cheyenne): Attorney/businessman; U.S. Attorney for the District of Wyoming, 2026–Present. Won the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State Senate District 6 with 37.7% of the vote. Unopposed in the Nov. 5, 2024 general election.

Republican · Cheyenne, Laramie County

Primary result: Won, 1,780 votes (37.7%), 1st of 6.

General result: State Senate SD06, Republican, 8,484 votes (100%), Unopposed.

OccupationAttorney/businessman
Background

Darin Smith was born in Rock Springs, Wyoming. He graduated from Rock Springs High School. He earned a bachelor's and a law degree from the University of Wyoming in 1996 and 2000, respectively. His career experience includes working as an attorney and businessman.

I'm Darin Smith, a fourth-generation Wyomingite from Rock Springs, now living in Cheyenne with my wife Alicia and our family. At the University of Wyoming, I wrestled proudly as a Cowboy and earned Honorable Mention Academic All-American honors in 1994 and 1995. Since 2005, I've served as an executive and consultant with nonprofits like the Christian Broadcasting Network and Family Research Council, advocating nationwide for faith, family, and freedom. Maintaining a successful law practice since 2000, I've represented individuals, families, and corporations, prioritizing access to justice. …

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Preserving Wyoming's core values and tackling our current challenges head-on. Reducing Taxes, Protecting our energy sector, maintaining fiscal discipline, and defending our Constitutional Rights.

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

I firmly uphold the principle of local control, rooted in the belief that decisions are best made closest to the people they affect. Local leaders understand their communities' needs better than distant bureaucrats in Cheyenne. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

In housing, reducing regulatory barriers encourages competitive pricing and innovation, expanding access to affordable homes. Healthcare should emphasize consumer-driven options, fostering competition among providers to lower costs and improve quality of care. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

I'm committed to conservative principles and believe in listening to everyone's views, but when it comes to God, Family, and Country, there's no room for compromise.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

I prioritize responsible resource management that respects private property rights and promotes economic growth.
