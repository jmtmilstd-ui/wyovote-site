Kevin Skates

Kevin Skates (Republican, Thermopolis): Career in retail and public service, including work at Hot Springs State Park in Thermopolis; has served as president of the Thermopolis Rotary Club, the Hot Springs County Travel and Tourism Board, and the Wyoming Recreation and Parks Association (self-described, 2024). Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 28 with 36.5% of the vote.

Republican · Thermopolis, Hot Springs County

Primary result: Lost, 980 votes (36.5%), 2nd of 2.

Research

OccupationCareer in retail and public service, including work at Hot Springs State Park in Thermopolis; has served as president of the Thermopolis Rotary Club, the Hot Springs County Travel and Tourism Board, and the Wyoming Recreation and Parks Association (self-described, 2024)
Background

Born in Cody and grew up in Wyoming; a self-described lifelong Wyoming Republican. In 2014 he was awarded the Reach Award for expertise in tourism and hospitality by Gov. Matt Mead. He said his career in retail and public service was spent working with people and businesses to improve quality of life, and that he chose to remain in Wyoming because of its people and quality of life.

EducationUniversity of Wyoming, degree in parks and recreation administration (self-described, 2024)
Positions

Borders, energy and tourism

In a 2024 candidate-submitted biography he said Wyoming needs representation that will “garner our Wyoming values,” and that to improve economic success and public safety “we must secure our national borders,” quoting Ronald Reagan. He said it is crucial to continue pushing forward with oil, gas, coal, and uranium, and to promote tourism — “our second largest industry” — while preserving Wyoming tradition.

Economic development and outdoor recreation

At a July 2024 Thermopolis-Hot Springs Chamber forum he said oil, gas, and coal are cyclical, while tourism and the state park have been “rock solid,” and that Wyoming should expand outdoor recreation — cattle rides, rock climbing, fishing, and open spaces — to bring visitors who spend money. He said consultants had called Thermopolis’s potential “crazy good,” citing hot springs, world-class fishing, dinosaurs, history, and the canyon.

FundraisingRaised $10,980. Spent $6,692.46. 2023-2024 election cycle (contributions tagged to the 2024 cycle in WyCFIS), through Sep. 4, 2024.
