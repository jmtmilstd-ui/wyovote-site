Background

In her 2024 WyoFile questionnaire, Gail Symons wrote that she lived in the house she purchased from her parents 14 years earlier, adjacent to the Symons family ranch eight miles east of Sheridan, and that the family is in its fourth and fifth generation of Wyoming agriculture, with a land patent predating statehood. She wrote that she was born and raised in Sheridan, would be 68 on Election Day 2024, and had been a registered Sheridan County Republican voter for 50 years.

She wrote that she earned a B.S. in marketing at the University of Wyoming, then joined the U.S. Navy Supply Corps, with shipboard tours and assignments in retail, finance, auditing, and program management, and concurrently earned an M.B.A. in organizational management at the University of Hawaii. After retiring from the Navy she joined General Electric in its Six Sigma program and later worked in marketing and supply chain. In 2016 she retired again and moved back to Sheridan full time. She wrote that she created Civics307.com to track legislation and established WY Vote (wyvote.vote) to help inform voters.

Issue positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

The greatest resources we have available are the people, organizations and institutions within the state. Tapping into their knowledge and expertise to develop and evaluate alternatives through frank and open discussions that respect differing points of view will be the most effective path to meeting those challenges …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

The Home Rule Amendment to the Wyoming Constitution passed in November of 1972 with more than 75% voting Aye. While the state Supreme Court has not acknowledged the shift in power of the amendment, the legislators’ oath to the state constitution must accommodate the intention of the people. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

In keeping with the basic principles of freedom and free markets, the role of the state in supporting the well-being of citizens must determine how any program or service would preserve the first and consider the second. Our low population severely hinders viable free market solutions to citizen needs. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

The late Senator Mike Enzi spoke often about how to move forward in governance by finding common ground on the 80% or so that people can agree upon and avoiding the 20% that is contentious. …

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

Our County Commissioners are extremely effective in creating balance through their statutorily mandated comprehensive land use plans. Each of the 23 counties differ in the natural resources, wildlife and open spaces. …
