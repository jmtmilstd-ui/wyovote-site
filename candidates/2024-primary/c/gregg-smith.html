Gregg Smith

Profile · 2024 Primary

Gregg Smith (Republican, Cheyenne). Withdrew from the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State Senate District 4 after filing.

Primary result: Withdrawn, 1,059 votes. Withdrew after ballots were printed; votes shown as "* Withdrawn Candidate" in the official summary.

Research

ResidenceNorth Cheyenne, Laramie County (moved to Cheyenne in 2005; 2024 WyoFile questionnaire)
Background

Gregg Smith withdrew from the 2024 Republican primary for Senate District 4. In his 2024 WyoFile questionnaire he wrote that he lived in north Cheyenne, had lived in his then-current residence three years, had moved to Cheyenne in 2005 after leaving the Army, was born in Eugene, Oregon, and would be 51 on Election Day 2024.

He wrote that he has a bachelor's degree in marketing and an M.B.A. in operations and project management (he did not name the institutions), served about 7½ years in the Army before leaving because of injury, and had also taught at the community level in Wyoming. No more specific current occupation was given in the questionnaire.

Issue positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Communication, clarity and explanation. If people know the how, why, and end product of what we are doing, they will be more likely to support our decisions. Tourism, travel, and rodeo have the potential to be huge draws in Wyoming and I don’t think we, as a state, have taken full advantage of that opportunity. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

Our state is so diverse it’s hard to ask a small group of law makers to make all those decisions. For example, what is good for Jackson won’t necessarily be good for Cheyenne. Whenever possible, governing should be done locally, within the boundaries set by the state.

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

Our citizens come first and ideas can come from anywhere. Whether it’s from a legislature or a custodian working a night shift. Solutions to these problems are out there, we just have look past party lines and social status to find them.

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

Without communication and compromise at some level, nothing will get accomplished. I can bend as long as it’s in the best interest of our state.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

We could start by redeveloping abandoned properties. Tearing down derelict buildings to allow for new development or reclamation for wildlife. Revisiting ordinances on properties to make maximum use of developed areas. Find ways to make energy producing areas more wildlife friendly.
