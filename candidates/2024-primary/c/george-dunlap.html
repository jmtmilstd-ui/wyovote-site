George Dunlap

George Dunlap (Republican, Gillette). Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 32 with 39.9% of the vote.

Republican · Gillette, Campbell County

Primary result: Lost, 706 votes (39.9%), 2nd of 2.

Research

Background

In my childhood I lived on a ranch south of Gillette. Wyoming roots run deep in my family, as my grandfather and great-grandfather homesteaded the ranch in 1914. Early in my career, I established a successful photography business in 1977. The business created the opportunity to come to know one of the greatest aspects of Wyoming: the people. Through my profession I was able to build long-lasting relationships with the community members. While photography was my main profession, I have also been the owner of numerous successful businesses. Some of the life experiences, includes being a member of the Rotary Club, and an active member of New Life Wesleyan Church. …

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Property Tax is the biggest issue facing all of the people in Wyoming. I would support rolling the taxes back to 2020 levels. Installing aa aquation value basis for new property owners and cap all increases to the CPI for the State of Wyoming. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

Very simple, in the Wyoming State Constitution all guidelines are laid out. If we follow them, we will be golden.

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

In Gillette most of our housing problems have been caused the over regulation of the building codes. It has driven most of the building company's out of town to other places. We need a balance of proper codes and reasonable construction. Getting back to the basics in education is very important. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

We have to work for the common goal of making the State a better place to live.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

With all the open spaces in Wyoming we have room for them all. Poor planning is the downfall of most of our problems. There is plenty of room, proper planning will produce success for all of them.
