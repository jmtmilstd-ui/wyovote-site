Thomas D. Crank

Thomas D. Crank (Republican, Diamondville): Registered professional civil engineer and land surveyor; longtime owner of an engineering business in Kemmerer (self-described, 2020); Wyoming House of Representatives District 18, 2017–2021. Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 18 with 38.1% of the vote.

Republican · Diamondville, Lincoln County

Primary result: Lost, 733 votes (38.1%), 2nd of 2.

Background

Republican candidate for Wyoming House District 18 representative. Lifelong Wyo. resident, UW graduate, professional engineer and land surveyor. President of Eng. firm for 29 years and retired after 42 years. Served on numerous local boards including Lin. Co. P & Z Comm., and as state representative for Wyoming House District 18 from 2016 to 2020. I am an advocate for economic development, limited government, local control, property tax reform, private property rights, public lands in public hands, second amendment rights, the bill of rights, investing in public infrastructure including use of more water from the Green River. …

Lifetime Wyoming resident and Kemmerer engineer. In a 2020 candidate profile he said he was a registered professional civil engineer and land surveyor, had been in the engineering business in Kemmerer for more than 38 years, and had owned and operated the business for more than 26 years. He previously served six years as a Lincoln County representative on the Upper Green River Basin Joint Powers Board. Represented House District 18 from Jan. 10, 2017, to Jan. 4, 2021. Wikipedia lists his birthplace as Laramie, Wyoming, and his year of birth as 1960.

EducationUniversity of Wyoming graduate; Wyoming public-school educated (self-described, 2020)

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Wyoming's biggest challenge going forward is its tax structure. It's been the same problem for the last decade. Everyone needs to contribute to the solution and execrative industries cannot be asked to carry the load. The only real options are property tax and sales tax. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

I am an advocate for local control. When the state starts denying local control, I get uneasy. My guide here is, does this issue treat local governments differently than it would private individuals. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

Concerning economic development, the state's role should be consistent and limited regulation and taxation. Concerning housing, development of property is driven by projected profits. It's the American way. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

I don't know the background with most issues. I am always willing to present by perspective but often find insight from others and if their information is credible and compelling then sure compromise is called for. We all come with different perspectives and the legislature needs to be a balance of them.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

House District 18 is home to vast spaces and world-renowned wildlife and recreational areas, much of it on public land. At the same time nearly all the trona, large natural gas fields, processing plants and coal mines exist. …
