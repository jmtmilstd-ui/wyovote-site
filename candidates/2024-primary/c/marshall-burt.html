Marshall Burt

Marshall Burt (Republican, Green River): Career experience includes working as a track inspector with Union Pacific; Wyoming House of Representatives District 39, 2021–2023; U.S. Marine Corps. Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 39 with 8.4% of the vote.

Republican · Green River, Sweetwater County

Primary result: Lost, 88 votes (8.4%), 3rd of 3.

Occupationcareer experience includes working as a track inspector with Union Pacific.
Background

Marshall Burt was born in Rochester, Minnesota. Burt served in the U.S. Marine Corps. His career experience includes working as a track inspector with Union Pacific.

Born May 6, 1976, in Rochester, Minnesota. A Marine Corps veteran (1998–2007, staff sergeant) who later worked in Wyoming oilfields beginning in 2009 and then as a Union Pacific track inspector. He has also described himself as a part-time firefighter and a charter member of the Sweetwater County Marine Corps League, and said he helped build the American Legion home in Green River. He served in the Wyoming House from District 39 from 2021 to 2023 as a Libertarian — the first third-party legislator elected in Wyoming in more than 100 years — and ran in 2024 as a Republican.

Positions

Budget and spending (2024 debate)

At an Aug. 6, 2024, Sweetwater GOP debate he called for regular audits of agencies and criticized a $150 million high-school expenditure as unnecessary spending, saying the money is “not free” and is paid through inflation at the gas station and grocery store.

Economy, taxes and education (2020 candidate Q&A)

In a 2020 SweetwaterNOW questionnaire, when he first ran for the House, he said he wanted to be a voice for economic diversity by opening markets, to let people keep more of their money to invest in their communities, and to “invite innovation to education to improve student success.” He called the budget the most important issue, said accounts losing tens of millions made tax increases irresponsible, and favored better investment management, new industries, and fuller use of natural resources.
