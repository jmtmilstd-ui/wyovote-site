Charles K. Scott

Charles K. Scott (Republican, Casper): Cattle rancher; Wyoming House, 1979–1982; Wyoming Senate District 30, 1983–Present. Won the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State Senate District 30 with 47.8% of the vote. Unopposed in the Nov. 5, 2024 general election.

Republican · Casper, Natrona County

Primary result: Won, 1,438 votes (47.8%), 1st of 3.

General result: Unopposed, 6,406 votes (100%), 1st of 1.

Research

OccupationCattle rancher
Background

Scott earned his B.A. from Harvard College in 1967. He went on to receive his MBA from Harvard Business School in 1969. Scott has worked as a cattle rancher.

Education: BA Harvard College, MBA Harvard Business School, designated a George F. Baker Scholar (top 5% of class). My first job after graduation was an operations research analyst in Washington, D.C. I have maintained those skills and found them very useful as a member of the Wyoming Legislature. Principal Occupation: Cattle Rancher. In 1971 my family bought a very run down ranch. I came back to Wyoming to run it in 1974 and was joined by my brother a couple of years later. Restoring the ranch to profitable productivity has been a very satisfying life work. I have met the test of success in our private free enterprise system. …

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

The key is to get our budget and financial systems right. Our basic services need to be well funded so we do a good job providing them. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

The general rule should be to favor local control. Better decisions are made if local circumstances are considered. Two exceptions. Sometimes a uniform state rule will make private businesses more efficient saving us all money. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

The state should spend enough so our basic governmental services are well done. The state appropriately provides partial funding for amenities- fine and performing arts, outdoor recreation, etc. Wyoming is not building enough housing to meet the needs of young families. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

The art of compromise is the art of good legislation. The divided system of government that our founding fathers gave us is designed to force compromise. With the diversity of interests and opinions present in our free society our founders were very wise in this design. …

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

In general our system is currently doing a relatively good job of balancing these issues. There will always be debates over specific issues. For example, I oppose the proposed Coates Road gravel pit. It hurts too many people. Access to public lands is becoming a major issue. …
