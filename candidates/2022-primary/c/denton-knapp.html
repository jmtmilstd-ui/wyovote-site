Denton Knapp

Denton Knapp (Republican, Gillette): As of June 2022, administrative director for the Campbell County Board of Commissioners. Earlier in the campaign he had launched Valor Made, LLC, in Gillette, hiring veterans for oil-field and other work. Previously director of veteran services for Goodwill Industries of Orange County after retiring from the Army as a colonel in 2017; U.S. Army, 30 years, retired as a colonel in 2017, including multiple combat tours in Iraq and Afghanistan. After retirement he served in the California State Guard (Cowboy State Daily also described that role as brigadier general) and as director of veteran services for Goodwill Industries of Orange County. Lost the Aug. 16, 2022 Republican primary for U.S. Representative with 1.3% of the vote.

Republican · Gillette, Campbell County

Primary result: Lost, 2,258 votes (1.3%), 4th of 5.

Background

Raised in Gillette; his mother Waruny is a Japanese immigrant and his younger brother is state Rep. Christopher Knapp of Gillette (a different candidate from this U.S. House race). Married to Heather, a Gillette native, for 35 years as of February 2022. After retiring from the Army he lived in Orange County, California, before moving back to Gillette in 2021. In June 2022 he took a full-time job as administrative director for the Campbell County commissioners while remaining in the race.

EducationAppointed to the U.S. Military Academy at West Point, nominated by then-Rep. Dick Cheney and Sens. Al Simpson and Malcolm Wallop; Cowboy State Daily reported he had been accepted by all three service academies and that the West Point appointment began his Army career. A degree year is not stated in those articles.
MilitaryU.S. Army, 30 years, retired as a colonel in 2017, including multiple combat tours in Iraq and Afghanistan. After retirement he served in the California State Guard (Cowboy State Daily also described that role as brigadier general) and as director of veteran services for Goodwill Industries of Orange County.
Positions

Federal government and immigration

Announcing the campaign in May 2021, he said he wanted to restore trust in elected officials and in the election process, criticized then-Rep. Liz Cheney’s vote to impeach Donald Trump as out of step with constituents, and said the federal immigration system was “broken,” adding that his mother was from Japan and became a naturalized citizen “after much hard work” and that “there has to be a process.”
