John B. Romero-Martinez

John B. Romero-Martinez (Republican, Cheyenne): Communications and cybersecurity specialist; in a 2020 candidate profile he described his work as general labor and general tech work; Wyoming House of Representatives District 44, 2021–2023; U.S. Air Force National Guard, communications, five years. Lost the Aug. 16, 2022 Republican primary for State House District 44 with 30.6% of the vote.

Republican · Cheyenne, Laramie County

Primary result: Lost, 293 votes (30.6%), 2nd of 3.

Background

John Romero-Martinez was born in Cheyenne, Wyoming.

Cheyenne native who represented House District 44 from 2021 to 2023, including service on the Labor, Health and Social Services and Transportation, Highways and Military Affairs committees. In 2020 he described his work as general labor and general tech work; in 2026 Wyoming Public Media, citing his LinkedIn profile, described him as a communications and cybersecurity specialist. He has also listed work as a legislative intern and Joan Snow Memorial Scholar at the Wyoming Legislature, as a cannabis-policy communications director, and two summers as a museum worker/curator assistant for the Department of State Parks and Historic Sites. He served five years in the Air Force National Guard in communications and spent five years on the U.S. Commission on Civil Rights Wyoming Advisory Committee.

EducationAssociate of Arts in Sociology, with emphasis in political science, anthropology, and criminal justice (Laramie County Community College). Also lists a People’s Law School diploma in general law from the Wyoming Trial Lawyers Association and additional coursework in music, dance, graphic design, genealogy, web and network development, and cybersecurity.
MilitaryU.S. Air Force National Guard, communications, five years

Positions

Limited government and school choice (2020 campaign)

In a 2020 candidate profile he said he believed in limited government, school choice, and that leaders should serve people rather than special interests. He also wrote that helping “the least of these” should not be a partisan issue, and that as a Catholic he believed “a life matters from beginning to end.”
