Paul Vogelheim

Paul Vogelheim (Republican, Jackson): Retired businessman with a background in international manufacturing; former Teton County commissioner (more than a decade, including as chair); Teton County, Wyoming, Board of Commissioners. Unopposed in the Aug. 16, 2022 Republican primary for State House District 23. Lost the Nov. 8, 2022 general election with 48.3% of the vote.

Republican · Jackson, Teton County

Primary result: Unopposed, 2,376 votes (100%), 1st of 1.

General result: State House HD23, Republican, 2,326 votes (48.3%), Lost.

Research

OccupationRetired businessman with a background in international manufacturing; former Teton County commissioner (more than a decade, including as chair)
Background

Lives north of Jackson with his two stepdaughters. Besides more than a decade on the Teton County Board of Commissioners, his 2022 announcement listed board service with the Community Foundation of Jackson Hole (executive committee), One-22 Resource Center and Jackson Cupboard, St. John’s Health Foundation, The Center for the Secret of Peace (Rwanda), ConservAmerica, and Jackson Hole Friends of Scouting. KHOL described him as a moderate Republican and former commissioner with a history in international manufacturing.

Prior officesTeton County, Wyoming, Board of Commissioners
Positions

Infrastructure, housing, taxes and conservation

In his announcement he said Teton County needs a voice to advocate for schools, hospitals, roads and bridges, champion small businesses, help facilitate creative solutions to the housing crisis, and protect the county’s “parallel priorities of community and conservation.” He listed challenges including property-tax increases, lack of affordable workforce housing, behavioral-health access, and the loss of teachers to the high cost of living. At a September 2022 forum he opposed a real estate transfer tax, favored public-private partnerships for housing, and supported a 5% cap on property taxes “across the board.”

Abortion and party role

At the same forum he said he supports a woman’s right to choose but was more open to restrictions than his Democratic opponent, including fighting for exceptions for rape and incest. He argued he would have more impact in Cheyenne as a Republican able to sit in the majority caucus and potentially chair committees such as Travel, Recreation, Wildlife and Cultural Resources.
