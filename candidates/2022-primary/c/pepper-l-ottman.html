Pepper L. Ottman

Pepper L. Ottman (Republican, Riverton): Para professional, retired (Wyoming Legislature 2026 profile). Campaign biography also describes work as a bookkeeper, motor-vehicle title clerk, travel-agency agent, Pennsylvania state government employee, cafe proprietor, and 15 years in public education; Wyoming House District 34, 2021–Present; Fremont County Republican Party chairwoman (two terms, self-reported on 2021 campaign site). Unopposed in the Aug. 16, 2022 Republican primary for State House District 34. Unopposed in the Nov. 8, 2022 general election.

Republican · Riverton, Fremont County

Primary result: Unopposed, 2,797 votes (100%), 1st of 1.

General result: State House HD34, Republican, 3,407 votes (100%), Unopposed.

Background

Moved to Fremont County with her husband Larry in 1998 and has lived in House District 34 since (26 years as of the 2024 campaign site). Served as Fremont County Republican chair for four years and as state representative beginning in 2021. Campaign biography lists memberships including the Shoshoni Rifle Club, Wyoming Gun Owners, NRA, Lighthouse Bible Church, Fremont County Library System, and Prison Fellowship, and work as coordinator for Fremont County Wreaths Across America.

EducationTravel/Recreation/Hospitality Management Mesa State; General CWC 2013
Prior officesWyoming House District 34, 2021–Present; Fremont County Republican Party chairwoman (two terms, self-reported on 2021 campaign site); Fremont County Republican precinct committeewoman (self-reported, more than 10 years as of 2021)
Positions

Right to bear arms

Called the right to bear arms a fundamental right that protects other rights and said she would defend and work to expand Second Amendment protections.

Private property rights

Said she would defend the right to keep government from telling people what to do with their property.

Right to life

Said she would stand up for the sanctity of life, from conception to natural death.

Education

Supports keeping education control local so parents can make decisions about their own children’s education.

Taxes and spending

Said the state must decrease spending, decrease taxes, and balance the budget; in 2024 she called tax reform paramount and tax relief a bandage until government funding in a downturn is decided.

2024 campaign priorities

Cited inflation in food and fuel prices, medical freedom, private property rights, federal overreach on public lands and grazing, and “the weaponization of water and land use.”
