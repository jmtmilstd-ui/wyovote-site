Leesa Kuhlmann

Leesa Kuhlmann (Democratic, Rock Springs): Teacher of children and adults for more than 40 years (described in 2022 coverage as a public instructor / retired educator). State secretary-treasurer of the Wyoming Education Association for Retired Members at the time of the campaign. Unopposed in the Aug. 16, 2022 Democratic primary for State Senate District 13. Lost the Nov. 8, 2022 general election with 24.0% of the vote.

Democratic · Rock Springs, Sweetwater County

Primary result: Unopposed, 385 votes (100%), 1st of 1.

General result: State Senate SD13, Democratic, 1,220 votes (24%), Lost.

OccupationTeacher of children and adults for more than 40 years (described in 2022 coverage as a public instructor / retired educator). State secretary-treasurer of the Wyoming Education Association for Retired Members at the time of the campaign.
Background

A Wyoming native who said she came from the northwest corner of the state, she had taught for more than 40 years and described herself as a lifelong learner. The Rocket Miner listed membership in WEA-R (state secretary-treasurer), the Wyoming Music Educators Association, Wyoming Jazz Educators, Sweetwater Education of Retired Teachers, and Mount of Olives Lutheran Church. Married to Kurt for 39 years as of 2022, with a son and a daughter living in Wyoming.

EducationB.A. in music education, a bachelor’s degree in theory and music composition, and an M.A. in music education, all from the University of Wyoming; also attended Brigham Young University, the University of Northern Colorado, Colorado State University, and Drake University (Iowa).
Positions

Representation, economic development and energy

Her campaign theme was “reasonable, respectful, responsible representation.” She said a one-party system was not working, that Republicans had tried to “jerrymander Democrats out of existence,” and that Wyoming needed to “get back to the center.” She wanted to advocate for Sweetwater County and the I-80 corridor, put empty warehouses to work, explore solar and wind and lithium-related opportunities, and said “we need to wean ourselves from oil and gas.” The Green River Star also reported she favored expanding Medicare, diversifying economic development, and using reserve (“rainy day”) funds for school funding, saying “it’s raining,” and that teachers should be trusted as professionals.
