Gordon sends his final supplemental budget to lawmakers as December hearings near

News

By WyoVote

Week of Nov 23, 2026

Gov. Mark Gordon released a $527 million supplemental budget request on Nov. 16. The Joint Appropriations Committee hears it Dec. 7-11. Secretary of State-elect Robert Short named a deputy on Nov. 17.

CHEYENNE — Gov. Mark Gordon has sent lawmakers his last budget request before he leaves office. Committees are finishing their interim work. New statewide officers are building their staffs.

The supplemental budget

Gordon released his supplemental budget request on Nov. 16, 2026.1, 2 It asks for about $527 million.2 A supplemental budget adds to the two-year budget that lawmakers approved in spring 2026. Gordon said the request focuses on “emergent, unanticipated, or obligatory expenses” and on finishing work already under way.1, 2 The Legislature will take it up in the 2027 General Session.2

Items in the request include:

  • $16.6 million to keep property tax relief programs running through June 2028. The current budget pays for them only through June 2027.2
  • $100 million added to permanent savings.1, 2
  • $40.4 million for the University of Wyoming, including money to finish two new dorms.2
  • $31.6 million for schools, as a legislative committee recommended.2
  • $30 million through the Wyoming Wildlife and Natural Resource Trust for invasive species, wildfire mitigation and recovery work.1, 2
  • More money for Medicaid waiver programs, for people who have waited more than two years for help and for caregiver pay rates.1
  • $436,000 one-time and $575,000 for the state’s share of the SUNBucks summer nutrition program’s administrative costs.2
  • $500,000 for litigation support on water, export access and other state interests.1

Gordon leaves office on Jan. 4, 2027, when Governor-elect Eric Barlow is inaugurated.9

Legislative committees

The Management Council meets Nov. 23 in Cheyenne. Its agenda lists the 2027 General Session calendar, reports from the Select Committee on Gaming and the Health Insurance Affordability Task Force, and updates to legislative rules.3 The Legislature’s calendar lists Nov. 26 as a state holiday.4

The Joint Revenue Committee meets Nov. 30-Dec. 1 to finish its interim work.4 Its bill drafts include residential property tax bills, one of them a constitutional amendment, a homeowner property tax exemption, motor vehicle property tax relief, and the taxation of data centers.4, 5

The Joint Appropriations Committee meets Dec. 7-11. Gordon is scheduled to present the supplemental budget on Dec. 7. Agencies testify after him. Public comment on agency requests is taken on Friday, Dec. 11.6

Secretary of State transition

Secretary of State-elect Robert Short announced on Nov. 17 that he will hire Cara Boyle Chambers as deputy secretary of state.7 Chambers directs the Wyoming Attorney General’s Division of Victim Services.7 As deputy, she will oversee functions such as elections and business filings.7 Short takes office in January. Secretary of State Chuck Gray won Wyoming’s seat in the U.S. House on Nov. 3.7
