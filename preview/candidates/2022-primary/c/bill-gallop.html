Bill Gallop

Bill Gallop (Republican, Cheyenne): Investment management professional. His 2022 campaign site said he led about $4 billion in global-equity portfolio investments as senior investment officer at the Wyoming Retirement System, taught a University of Wyoming College of Business adjunct course on futures and options, spent three decades in investment management and consulting including as an investment director at large U.S. foundations, endowments, and public pension systems, and worked on Wall Street as a vice president at a major U.S. banking institution; U.S. Army Field Artillery, commissioned officer, attaining the rank of captain; graduate of U.S. Army Ranger, Parachutist, and Air Assault schools (self-described on his 2022 campaign website). Lost the Aug. 16, 2022 Republican primary for State Treasurer with 29.4% of the vote.

Republican · Cheyenne, Laramie County

Primary result: Lost, 40,658 votes (29.4%), 2nd of 2.

Background

The 2022 campaign site identified him as a U.S. military veteran and investment professional with Wyoming Retirement System and University of Wyoming teaching experience, a West Point computer-science degree (1986), a Cornell M.B.A. in finance, and the CFA designation.

EducationB.S. in computer science, U.S. Military Academy at West Point, 1986; M.B.A. (finance), Cornell University S.C. Johnson Graduate School of Management; Chartered Financial Analyst (CFA). The campaign site also said he received a congressional nomination to West Point.
MilitaryU.S. Army Field Artillery, commissioned officer, attaining the rank of captain; graduate of U.S. Army Ranger, Parachutist, and Air Assault schools (self-described on his 2022 campaign website).
Positions

State investments and fiscal conservatism

His 2022 campaign site argued that Wyoming needed “seasoned, proven investment management leadership” in the treasurer’s office and a more flexible approach to investing state financial assets after decades of falling interest rates ended. It described him as a constitutional and fiscal conservative, saying limited government and an unsustainable national debt burden were central concerns.
