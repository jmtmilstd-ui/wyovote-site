Jeremiah Hardesty

Jeremiah Hardesty (Republican, Bedford): United States Marine Corps, 2011–2016. His 2022 campaign biography also says he “went to Afghanistan from 2018–2020 to provide security.”. Lost the Aug. 16, 2022 Republican primary for State House District 21 with 41.4% of the vote.

Republican · Bedford, Lincoln County

Primary result: Lost, 1,235 votes (41.4%), 2nd of 2.

Research

Background

His 2022 campaign biography says he spent five years as a toddler in Kyiv, Ukraine, after the fall of the Soviet Union, speaking Ukrainian, Russian, and English while his family did humanitarian and church work; was a high school valedictorian; attended college on scholarship; served in the Marine Corps from 2011 to 2016; and later worked in Afghanistan from 2018 to 2020 providing security, picking up some Dari. An SVI June 6, 2022 interview page exists but is audio-only with no published transcript.

EducationHigh school graduate, class valedictorian (3.9 GPA, school not named); attended college at 18 on full scholarships (institution not named on his 2022 campaign biography).
MilitaryUnited States Marine Corps, 2011–2016. His 2022 campaign biography also says he “went to Afghanistan from 2018–2020 to provide security.”
Positions

Life, health freedom, and parents’ rights

His 2022 campaign platform called for a state-level role in ending abortion, opposed COVID-era business, church, and school shutdowns and “vaccine identification, forced mandates, and segregation,” and said parents should control their children’s education. Core values listed included the Second Amendment, local and state power, capitalism rather than “crony capitalism,” and “fiscal and financial responsibility through low taxes and transparent budgeting.”

Libraries and schools

On his issues page he criticized Lincoln County Library holdings he considered inappropriate for children, and he said he would have supported bills to restrict classroom teaching of critical race theory and to bar boys from girls’ sports, criticizing then-Rep. Evan Simpson’s votes on those bills.
