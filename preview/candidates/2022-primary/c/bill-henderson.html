Bill Henderson

Bill Henderson (Republican, Cheyenne): Business development executive, Cheyenne Market at FirsTier Bank; previously a chief financial officer, comptroller, and certified quality auditor (self-described, 2022); Wyoming House District 41, 2017–2024; Wyoming House of Representatives District 41, 2017–2025; U.S. Navy combat veteran, 26 years of service. Unopposed in the Aug. 16, 2022 Republican primary for State House District 41. Won the Nov. 8, 2022 general election with 49.4% of the vote.

Republican · Cheyenne, Laramie County

Primary result: Unopposed, 1,857 votes (100%), 1st of 1.

General result: State House HD41, Republican, 1,384 votes (49.4%), Won.

Background

Born and raised in Cheyenne; a married family man with children and grandchildren. In 2022 he was a sitting House District 41 representative (since 2017) and described himself as a business development executive at FirsTier Bank’s Cheyenne Market and a combat Navy veteran with 26 years of service. He listed prior service as vice chairman of the House Revenue Committee and on Transportation and Minerals/Agriculture committees, and as a former Laramie County GOP Senate District 7 chairman.

EducationCheyenne East High School; B.S. in economics and M.P.A., University of Wyoming; M.B.A., Walker School of Business & Technology, Webster University; financial management certification, Naval Postgraduate School, Monterey, California; Certified Quality Auditor (American Society for Quality); journeyman machinist and pipefitter, Union Pacific Railroad
MilitaryU.S. Navy combat veteran, 26 years of service
Prior officesWyoming House District 41, 2017–2024; Wyoming House of Representatives District 41, 2017–2025
Positions

Role of government

Said he believes in limited government, liberty, individual responsibility, free markets and open competition, and “less regulation, not more.” He also said he opposes discrimination in any form and believes in equal pay for equal work.

Budget, jobs and services

Priorities included balancing the state budget and right-sizing government, broadening the revenue base, good-paying jobs, access to affordable health care (including long-term care and mental health), fully funded quality education and vocational training, public safety, and road and highway infrastructure. He said he planned legislation on a Purple Star program for military-connected students, a COLA discussion for state retirees, and a parity tax on digital streaming services to reclaim revenue no longer collected on physical media.
