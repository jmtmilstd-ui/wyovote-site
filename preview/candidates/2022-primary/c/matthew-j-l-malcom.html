Matthew J.L. Malcom

Matthew J.L. Malcom (Republican, Cheyenne): Wyoming Air National Guard member (enlisted full-time, later part-time); former legislative aide to the Wyoming Legislature; Wyoming Air National Guard; his campaign site said he enlisted full-time after graduation and later re-enlisted part-time to finish his education. A 2024 WTE profile said he had been in the Guard for six years, three of them full-time. Lost the Aug. 16, 2022 Republican primary for State House District 61 with 28.3% of the vote.

Republican · Cheyenne, Laramie County

Primary result: Lost, 377 votes (28.3%), 2nd of 3.

Research

OccupationWyoming Air National Guard member (enlisted full-time, later part-time); former legislative aide to the Wyoming Legislature
Background

Moved to Wyoming as a child because of his father’s military service. He wrote that he served as a legislative aide during his adolescence, then enlisted full-time in the Wyoming Air National Guard, later serving part-time to finish school at the University of Wyoming. A 2024 WTE profile also said he had spent two years as a legislative aide for Laramie County legislative leadership.

EducationCampaign website said he was slated to graduate in May 2025 from the University of Wyoming with a bachelor’s in political science and American history. A contemporaneous August 2024 Wyoming Tribune Eagle profile described him as pursuing a graduate degree at the University of Wyoming in American politics and as a full-time student for the prior two years.
MilitaryWyoming Air National Guard; his campaign site said he enlisted full-time after graduation and later re-enlisted part-time to finish his education. A 2024 WTE profile said he had been in the Guard for six years, three of them full-time.
Positions

Education

Said Wyoming’s constitution values education and that the tradition should continue through parental rights, a properly funded model, and retaining teachers who promote high learning outcomes.

Economy and civility

Cited inflation and housing issues in House District 61 and called for creative solutions for businesses and diversification. As a fiscal conservative he said internal legislative conflict costs the state money, and that civility and efficacy with time and funds should be priorities.
