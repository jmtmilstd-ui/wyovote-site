John B Romero-Martinez

John B Romero-Martinez (Republican, Cheyenne): Communications and cybersecurity specialist; in a 2020 candidate profile he described his work as general labor and general tech work; Wyoming House of Representatives District 44, 2021–2023; U.S. Air Force National Guard, communications, five years. Lost the Aug. 20, ate House District 44 with 12.3% of the vote.

Republican · Cheyenne, Laramie County

Primary result: Lost, 71 votes (12.3%), 3rd of 3.

Background

John Romero-Martinez was born in Cheyenne, Wyoming.

I am a dynamic individual working to continue to build relations within state public and private sectors with emphasis on upholding the supreme laws of the land! My background and experience as a non-profit, small business, public servant and elected leader - from high school to present has set new standards for true-representation and governance in Wyoming. My representation among diverse/vulnerable populations is second to none. I continue to collaborate with people in the district and state by focusing on the Principles/Pillar’s of: Dignity, Subsidiarity, Solidarity, and the Common Good. I strive to use my skills, knowledge, and abilities towards authentic/effective governance. …

Cheyenne native who represented House District 44 from 2021 to 2023, including service on the Labor, Health and Social Services and Transportation, Highways and Military Affairs committees. In 2020 he described his work as general labor and general tech work; in 2026 Wyoming Public Media, citing his LinkedIn profile, described him as a communications and cybersecurity specialist. He has also listed work as a legislative intern and Joan Snow Memorial Scholar at the Wyoming Legislature, as a cannabis-policy communications director, and two summers as a museum worker/curator assistant for the Department of State Parks and Historic Sites. He served five years in the Air Force National Guard in communications and spent five years on the U.S. Commission on Civil Rights Wyoming Advisory Committee.

EducationAssociate of Arts in Sociology, with emphasis in political science, anthropology, and criminal justice (Laramie County Community College). Also lists a People’s Law School diploma in general law from the Wyoming Trial Lawyers Association and additional coursework in music, dance, graphic design, genealogy, web and network development, and cybersecurity.
MilitaryU.S. Air Force National Guard, communications, five years

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

The supreme laws of the land must continue to be our guiding lights. In order to protect the full spectrum of right to life, liberty, property, and the pursuit of happiness I will continue to propose bills that strengthen our most cherished values. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

As a state lawmaker, I will work with senior leaders and all branches of government to ensure that scope of impact, expertise and resources, consistency and state laws, and public interests guide the conversation. Accountability and Efficiency must also guide our decisions. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

Come together and do the right thing ad pass the Medical Treatment Opportunities Act - and expand Medicaid, this will help all areas of the industry and save our state more money (about 5 billion to be exact) than any other single piece of legislation can. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

By talking to them, meeting with them, and doing it with proper legal guidance, the same way I went 8 for 8 with HB 161 and 162. Each situation will be different, communication is the key.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

Simply by working with subject matter experts and listening to the people and legal teams to ensure that balance is reached.
