Daniel J. Singh

Daniel J. Singh (Republican, Cheyenne): Customer Service; Wyoming House District 61, 2023–Present. Won the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 61 with 58.9% of the vote. Unopposed in the Nov. 5, 2024 general election.

Republican · Cheyenne, Laramie County

Primary result: Won, 506 votes (58.9%), 1st of 2.

General result: Unopposed, 2,516 votes (100%), 1st of 1.

Background

Daniel Singh was born in Torrington, Wyoming, and lives in Cheyenne, Wyoming. Singh graduated from East High School in 2015. He earned an associate degree from Calvary Chapel Bible College in York, England, in 2020. His career experience includes working in customer service. Singh was a member of the One Way Evangelistic Ministries.

I am a proud American and Wyomingite. I am a lifelong Resident of Wyoming. I am a first generation American, My father is an immigrant from Trinidad and Tobago in the Caribbean. I come from a missionary family and have experience Doing evangelical and humanitarian work across the globe. I was first elected at the age of 25, and I am an active member of both my community and local Republican party. I intentionally communicate with the Democrats as they have no formal representation from Laramie County. I am a tenor With the Cheyenne chamber singers. I am passionate about history, Political science, And philosophy. I love my country and my state and I am proud of them both.

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

If I identify a problem, it is my responsibility to address it. To tackle significant issues, I would engage with stakeholders who have diverse perspectives and foster open dialogue about the matter. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

In matters of individual liberties and rights, state statutes and the Constitution must be upheld as the ultimate authority to protect our freedoms. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

I believe people should have the freedom to pursue any opportunities they set their minds to, free from unnecessary government interference. To facilitate this, individuals should retain as much of their earnings as possible. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

My willingness to compromise depends on the issue at hand. On matters of natural rights, I will not budge. Freedom of speech, the right to bear arms, private property rights, and unenumerated rights are non-negotiable. …

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

Wildlife populations predate human settlement, and I believe it is humanity's responsibility to be good stewards of our wildlife populations. Beyond the obvious ecological benefits, there are significant economic considerations as well. Wyoming families often use more resources than they contribute to the economy. …
