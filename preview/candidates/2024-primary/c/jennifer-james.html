Jennifer James

Jennifer James (Republican, Cheyenne): Owner of a nonprofit healthcare training company; honeybee farm operator, Carpenter, Wyoming. Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 10 with 26.5% of the vote.

Republican · Cheyenne, Laramie County

Primary result: Lost, 546 votes (26.5%), 2nd of 2.

Research

OccupationOwner of a nonprofit healthcare training company; honeybee farm operator, Carpenter, Wyoming
Background

My formal education includes a doctoral degree and several master’s degrees; one in law. I completed a post-graduate certificate from the Kennedy School of Government at Harvard University in Public Policy Design. I am passionate about evidence-based processes. I plan to support and improve the education system in Wyoming, particularly in terms of funding and workforce development by utilizing science, not opinions, when evaluating new programs. I have experience in policy and rulemaking and was formerly employed by two state agencies.

I'm Jennifer James, owner of a non-profit healthcare training company. I registered as a Republican in Wyoming in 2016 after moving to Carpenter WY and opening a honeybee farm. My formal education includes a doctoral degree and several master's degrees; one in law. I completed a post-graduate certificate from the Kennedy School of Government at Harvard University in Public Policy Design. I have experience in policy and rulemaking and was formerly employed by two state agencies. I helped write draft bills and rules and testified on several topics.

EducationDoctoral degree and several master's degrees, including one in law; post-graduate certificate in Public Policy Design, Kennedy School of Government, Harvard University
Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

I will support efforts to improve healthcare access and affordability for residents of Laramie County by taking the personal time and resources to study issues brought forward with due diligence. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

In the Supreme Court case Milliken v. Bradley, the Court located the tradition of local control in the federal Constitution’s guarantees of substantive due process, the right to vote, federalism, and the separation of powers. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

Encouragement and support of the private sector in order to stimulate growth. We have too much state government.

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

In the past, I helped write draft bills and rules and testified on several topics. I was respected for my knowledge and developed friendly relationships with seated members and lobbyists. I would like to see deliberate decision-making in our Wyoming legislative body. …

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

No response.

Why are you running, and what would your legislative priorities be?

I am running for House District 10 because I am dedicated to serving the People as guided by our U.S. and Wyoming Constitutions. My Legislative priorities are the Wyoming budget, education system and healthcare system. I plan to support and improve the education system in Wyoming, particularly in terms of funding and workforce development by utilizing science, not opinions, when evaluating new programs. I will support efforts to improve healthcare access and affordability for residents of Laramie County. My goal is to learn the budget, adjust down the excess, and use the intricate rules of our Legislative process to make sound decisions. I do not accept funds from political action committees because I only work for the People of Wyoming.
