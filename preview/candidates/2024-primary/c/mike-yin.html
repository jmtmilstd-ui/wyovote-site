Mike Yin

Mike Yin (Democratic, Jackson): Software Developer; Wyoming House District 16, 2019–Present. Unopposed in the Aug. 20, 2024 Democratic primary for State House District 16. Unopposed in the Nov. 5, 2024 general election.

Democratic · Jackson, Teton County

Primary result: Unopposed, 469 votes (100%), 1st of 1.

General result: State House HD16, Democratic, 3,545 votes (100%), Unopposed.

Research

OccupationSoftware Developer
Background

Mike Yin earned a bachelor's degree in computer science from Carnegie Mellon University in 2007. Yin’s career experience includes working as a senior software developer with Android.

My full time job is writing software. Like others in Jackson, I moved for the access to the great outdoors, but stayed because I loved the community. My goal as an legislator has always been: How can I ensure folks in Wyoming can raise a family and find opportunity, have their kids grow up with a good education, and ensure that those kids have the chance to live and raise their own family in Wyoming.

EducationBachelor's: Carnegie Mellon University
Prior officesWyoming House District 16, 2019–Present
Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

I understand there are shared values regardless of party in Wyoming. We all care about our community, we all care about our freedoms, and we all care about the public land we all call our home and backyard. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

I trust communities want to make themselves better. All of them. We all see what's happening as young people leave in droves. As a state, we need to provide options for communities to be able to succeed. They know their problems, but often they are hamstrung with limited solutions. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

I look toward those values about being able to have children who they themselves can live and work in Wyoming. Young people see the lack of long term opportunity. They see their freedoms being taken away by the state. Our role as a state should be providing opportunity and protecting freedoms.

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

The legislature is a body made up of 62 Representatives and 31 Senators. Nothing can be done without at least 32 and 16 votes. I'm sent to Cheyenne to do the hard work with folks from all across the state.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

With grace and humility as I try to learn as much as possible to make the best decisions I can.
