Background

I was born and raised in Wyoming and I appreciate the culture, history, and traditions that are unique to Johnson County and our state. I am a recent graduate of The University of the Cumberlands with bachelor's degrees in both political science and history. I was also a Scholar All-American swimmer in college and my swimming career a. I've served two terms as a Republican Precinct Woman in Johnson County since elected in 2018. I am honored to also have been elected as a delegate at the Republican National Convention, representing the people of Johnson County. Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

It's not possible for any one legislator to pass a bill on their own, so, with that in mind, I would network with others to find where the most support is on topics that need addressed and are important to the people of my district and the state. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

The local government is the one closest to the people and therefore the one that best addresses their needs. The state has obvious state obligations such as state highways and other state infrastructure and constitutional obligations such as schools, but on topics that don't affect the state, I believe local decisions …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

These are very broad topics but the reason the government has wrestled with them for years is because the government is not necessarily suited at finding the best solutions. The one's best suited to address the well being of Wyoming citizens and their families are the individuals themselves. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

I believe Ronald Reagan's 80/20 rule, also followed by the late Senator Enzi, is the best way to get things done. The rule is as follows, "The person that agrees with you 80% of the time is a friend and ally, not a 20% traitor."

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

Everyone enjoys Wyoming's open spaces and I am committed to a no net loss of public lands. Much of our energy development has a temporary footprint and we need to make sure companies have the funds set aside to reclaim those lands when energy extraction is done. …
