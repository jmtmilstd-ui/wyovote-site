Robert L. Hendry

Robert L. Hendry (Republican, Lysite): Rancher; founder of an oilfield construction business (2001); Natrona County Commissioner; Natrona County Fire Protection District board. Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State Senate District 30 with 39.2% of the vote.

Republican · Lysite, Fremont County

Primary result: Lost, 1,179 votes (39.2%), 2nd of 3.

Research

OccupationRancher; founder of an oilfield construction business (2001)
Background

My website has a lot of this information. I have lobbied for numerous groups throughout the years-Wy Stock Growers Assn, Fire protection District, WY Business Alliance, the County County Commissioners Assn. I have served on numerous Governor appointed boards and feel that I can make a difference on day one.

Prior officesNatrona County Commissioner; Natrona County Fire Protection District board
Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

By working with State and Federal partners to ensure the viability of our legacy industries. I would provide the proper funding for the infrastructure that Wyoming needs ie roads, housing, transportation etc. Health care is going to be very challenging. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

All issues are not created equal but I would work with local school boards, county commissioners, city and town officials to come up with solutions that would enhance the prosperity and viability of local communities for the citizens of the state.

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

Housing and health care are going to be the two major issues in the next few years. We may need to pass legislation that supports all of our businesses and helps the citizens of Wyoming. We need to reduce regulations if that is truly a road block to housing and economic development. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

As a representative in state government, you have to be willing to compromise to get things done. I would listen to all sides of an issue and then vote on how I feel will best support my district and the state.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

This is a complicated issue. There is not a one size fits all solution. Wyoming wants their open spaces and an abundance of wildlife. We need energy development because they pay the bills in this state. …
