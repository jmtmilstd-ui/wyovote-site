Liz Storer

Liz Storer (Democratic, Jackson): Nonprofit executive; Wyoming House District 23, 2023–Present. Unopposed in the Aug. 20, 2024 Democratic primary for State House District 23. Unopposed in the Nov. 5, 2024 general election.

Background

Liz Storer lives in Jackson, Wyoming. Her career experience includes working as the leader of her family's foundation, the George B. Storer Foundation.

I was elected to HD 23 in 2022; I have engaged in policy issues in Wyoming for the last three decades. You can learn more about me at my website: storerforstatehouse.com.

EducationBA University of Southern California 1982; MFA University of Southern California 1984
Prior officesWyoming House District 23, 2023–Present
Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Wyoming's biggest challenge is the need to find a realistic transition from a fossil fuel-based economy. This will require making it possible for a more diverse economy to prosper and a tax policy that broadens our revenue sources in a way that is sustainable and equitable. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

Legislators claim to support local control until they don't, especially when it comes to local control in places like Teton County where legislators from elsewhere in the state feel they differ with our values or choose to listen to advocates for influential developers. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

Those four issues are key factors for whether people can and want to live in Wyoming: Can I afford a home and access to healthcare? Can my kids get a good education and find jobs? …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

Legislating successfully requires finding solutions that support your constituents' needs while recognizing other legitimate concerns and winning enough support to get enacted. Standing up for your values whenever possible is key. …

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

The work to map migration corridors for ungulates is a great example of identifying critical areas that wildlife rely on; we should have the political will to protect such habitats, recognizing that energy development can be accessed outside of those limited areas. …
