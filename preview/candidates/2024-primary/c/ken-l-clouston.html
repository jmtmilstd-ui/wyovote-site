Ken L. Clouston

Ken L. Clouston (Republican, Gillette): Physical Therapist; Wyoming House District 32, 2023–Present. Won the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 32 with 60.1% of the vote. Unopposed in the Nov. 5, 2024 general election.

Republican · Gillette, Campbell County

Primary result: Won, 1,062 votes (60.1%), 1st of 2.

General result: State House HD32, Republican, 3,342 votes (100%), Unopposed.

Background

Ken Clouston was born in Bismarck, North Dakota, and lives in Wyoming. Clouston earned a B.S. in physical therapy in 1992 and a doctorate of physical therapy in 2005, both from the University of North Dakota. His career experience includes working as a physical therapist.

My wife and I both have Doctor of Physical Therapy degrees, we opened our private practice in 2005 with one employee, and we now have over 20 employees and two clinics, Gillette and Casper. We live in Gillette year-round, and we have been married for 31 years, we have one incredible son. We have dedicated our lives to caring for and serving others. I have served as a volunteer and in elected positions at the local (Campbell County School Board x2 and our Healthcare Foundation), state (WY Legislature and WY State Board of Education, President and VP of the WY PT Association), and with federal affairs for the American Physical Therapy Association. …

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Politics, like life, is about trust and relationships. I take my responsibilities as a legislator very seriously and I am honored to represent my community. I will continue to respect all citizens and listen to both sides of an issue. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

I am a firm believer in local control. Just as I believe Washington D.C. should not dictate to Wyoming, I also believe that Cheyenne should not dictate to the rest of the state. What is best for Jackson, WY is not what is always best for Upton, Wyoming. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

Economic development is reliant on Wyoming having affordable housing, great health care, and great education. Wyoming has an incredible public education system. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

I believe that these traits, being able to respect everyone and learn from different perspectives, as some of my strongest attributes as a legislator. As a first-time legislator I was able to bring together and work with insurance companies, hospitals, and the Medical Association to lead the Prior Authorization bill. …

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

We have a responsibility to leave our wildlife treasures to our children and grandchildren. We cannot auction off Wyoming's open spaces and history for quick economic benefits. …
