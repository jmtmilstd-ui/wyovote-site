Jeffery Thomas

Jeffery Thomas (Republican, Guernsey): Fire chief of Guernsey, Wyoming; also described in 2024 coverage as a volunteer bodyguard for U.S. Senate candidate Reid Rasner; Fire Chief, Guernsey, Wyoming. Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 4 with 32.3% of the vote.

Republican · Guernsey, Platte County

Primary result: Lost, 804 votes (32.3%), 2nd of 2.

Research

OccupationFire chief of Guernsey, Wyoming; also described in 2024 coverage as a volunteer bodyguard for U.S. Senate candidate Reid Rasner
Background

Lifetime conservative, 36 years in business, 36 years in community service including Fire & EMS and other civic organizations

Fifth-generation Wyoming native with 36 years of public-service experience; his grandfather served in the Legislature in the late 1950s. A Platte County Record-Times announcement teaser also called him a lifelong civil servant. Guernsey fire chief who, as a volunteer, helped bodyguard U.S. Senate candidate Reid Rasner when Rasner was in the area, describing it as “just one friend helping out another.”

Prior officesFire Chief, Guernsey, Wyoming
Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

With common-sense and and equal representation for what's best for Wyoming

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

Most decisions should be made at the lowest level possible as long as they are legal and ethical

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

We should work towards what best for the citizens of Wyoming. We work for you not ourselves. Wyoming First

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

Diplomacy is the art of the possible. A good idea is a good idea no matter what your political affiliation is.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

That is a fine line but we need to fall on the side of what's best for Wyoming and its citizens
