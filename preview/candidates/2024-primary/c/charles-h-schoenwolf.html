Charles H Schoenwolf

Charles H Schoenwolf (Republican, Casper): Owner of businesses in construction, retail, and health-care services; work in long-term care, hospital, hospice and palliative care, and assisted living. Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State Senate District 30 with 13.1% of the vote.

Republican · Casper, Natrona County

Primary result: Lost, 394 votes (13.1%), 3rd of 3.

Research

OccupationOwner of businesses in construction, retail, and health-care services; work in long-term care, hospital, hospice and palliative care, and assisted living
Background

Construction, Nursing, Business Owner

My leadership experience has developed and evolved over the course of the last 40 years, involving the creation, development and maintaining of several personal businesses from construction, retail and health care services. My education in business and nursing from community Colleges and universities, my employment in long-term care, hospital, hospice and palliative care, assisted living and institutionalized care. I have also gained experience donating time to many different charities, the Rotary and was involved as a board member in our local community. I learned how to be a great follower, as well as a great leader. Teamwork, compassion and compromise are crucial. However, to never quit when fighting for what is right or against an injustice with unstaggering perseverance and resolve means everything. The Wyoming way of life is under attack at all levels, at all times and I plan to defend it for all of us.

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

My focus is to represent the needs and interest of the citizens, listen to the citizens and be their voice at the legislative level.

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

Following the State Constitution will be my guide.

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

The State is absolutely responsible for the above mentioned areas.

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

Compromise within reason is essential in dealing with issues and successfully passing statutes, as long as it doesn't Compromise values or integrity.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

It's essential to provide protections for wildlife and have a plan in place when developing in the above mentioned areas.

What committee assignments would you be most interested in and why?

Revenue / Property Taxes: My vote will always be NO to any increased taxes and I will support all reductions to Property Taxes. The Legislature is already preparing to find additional taxes to replace any reductions they give to citizens. The Legislature is discussing an increase to sales and use tax, creating an optional tax and suggesting to tax the sale of Real Estate. Oh, hell NO! K–12 Education and school choice, mental health/student well being, career and technical education, school based service programs, staff recruitment and retention are few of my interests. The Legislature really dropped the ball on not re-establishing The Wyoming Cowboy Challenge Academy for at-risk youths. The loss of the Challenge Academy has had a negative impact affecting hundreds of youth throughout the State. Seventy-five percent of the program was paid by the Wyoming National Guard, twenty-five percent by the State of Wyoming. The program helped students to graduate or earn GED Certificates, receive college scholarships and provided opportunities for technical job training. The Legislature needs to provide grants to help educate our youth about the dangers of drug and alcohol addiction and to provide additional mental health support. These are trying times and the State of Wyoming needs to be investing more into programs for the youth in our communities.

How would you prioritize capital spending/infrastructure in the state?

I fully support funding on maintaining needed infrastructure for roads, bridges and highways and equipment or services required throughout the State. However, I believe we need to keep a tight rein on spending when it comes to erecting new, multimillion-dollar buildings with chandeliers that cost tens of thousands of dollars at a time when the State can’t support needed services for its citizens. What is a state issue that you think is overlooked? Appropriation and a review of integrity and fairness in gaming. While gaming has become a billion-dollar-a-year industry in our State, helping to fill coffers, we can no longer walk into an establishment that isn’t packed full of slot machines. In communities already struggling to deal with alcohol and drug abuse, we have now added minimally regulated gambling. This issue has been overlooked by our Legislature and needs to be addressed expeditiously.

What can the Legislature do to improve public health?

Wyoming is eight years behind the curve on approving Medicaid Expansion. The bill passed the House of Representatives last session, but not the Senate. The State of Wyoming has lost millions and millions of dollars, resulting in an increasingly negative impact on our citizens, physicians and hospitals across the State by continuing not to come to terms with the issues surrounding the need for services these funds could provide. Nearly all states throughout the United States report a very positive impact after approving Medicaid expansion in their states. Local counties and the State of Wyoming should be working in collaboration with our hospitals on their needs to provide excellent care to our communities. Grants need to be established and in-depth discussions need to be happening. As a State Senator I will work to maintain a constant, open line of communication with our hospitals and their administrations. Healthcare Education and the welfare of Wyoming citizens is important for business development and the retention of our citizens. Please everyone make your vote count and vote in the Primary Election August 20, 2024.
