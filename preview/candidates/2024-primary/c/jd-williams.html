JD Williams

JD Williams (Republican, Lusk): Rancher; Wyoming House District 2, 2021–2022; Wyoming House District 2, 2025–Present. Won the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 2 with 54.8% of the vote. Unopposed in the Nov. 5, 2024 general election.

Republican · Lusk, Goshen, Niobrara, Weston Counties County

Primary result: Won, 1,528 votes (54.8%), 1st of 2.

General result: State House HD02, Republican, 3,834 votes (100%), Unopposed.

Background

J.D. Williams was born in Scottsbluff, Nebraska. Williams earned a B.S. in agricultural economics from the University of Wyoming in 1997. His career experience includes working as a rancher.

I am a rancher in northern Niobrara County who is a lifelong resident of Wyoming. I am thankful for my employees, my neighbors, and community leaders who have encouraged me to serve House District 2. Effectiveness has been a hallmark of my ranching career and I will serve House District 2 with the same focus.

EducationBachelor's: University of Wyoming, 1997
Prior officesWyoming House District 2, 2021–2022; Wyoming House District 2, 2025–Present
Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

Wyoming is under a lot of economic pressure from the Biden administration’s economic policies. Inflation is nationwide and in addition to that our core industries of coal, oil, and gas are being choked by the Biden administration’s permitting games. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

The less the Legislature overreaches into the area of local control the better. Bigger government is not the answer. Regardless, there are times when the Legislature must have the hard conversation and weigh local control against a pressing issue.

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

We must govern in a way that gives the next generation the opportunity to prosper. That usually means keeping government out of the way. These topics will continue to be huge challenges. The state cannot be the solution to every problem.

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

Dialogue and compromise have never been easy, but are foundational to our process. Anytime dissenting voices are excluded from our governing process, the chance of reaching a workable solution decreases. It has been said that "strength lies in differences, not in similarities."

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

Wyoming has always found solutions that strike a balance between the environment and the economy. Wyoming has the experts and the experience to continue to lead in this area.
