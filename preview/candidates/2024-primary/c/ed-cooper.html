Ed Cooper

Ed Cooper (Republican, Ten Sleep): Consultant; Wyoming Senate District 20, 2021–Present. Won the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State Senate District 20 with 52.2% of the vote. Unopposed in the Nov. 5, 2024 general election.

Republican · Ten Sleep, Big Horn, Fremont, Hot Springs, Park, Washakie Counties County

Primary result: Won, 2,652 votes (52.2%), 1st of 2.

General result: State Senate SD20, Republican, 8,236 votes (100%), Unopposed.

Background

Edward Cooper was born in Thermopolis, Wyoming. Cooper graduated from Ten Sleep High School in 1971. Cooper's career experience includes working as an oil and gas consultant.

Born and raised in the Big Horn Basin. I'm a lifelong Republican. My family first came to Wyoming in 1906. We have been involved in agriculture since. I have been in the energy business for 532 years, running my own service company for 43 years. My business experience extends all across north America and internationally.

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

I believe the biggest challenge to Wyoming today, on all fronts, is federal overreach. We will have to continue our fights against the EPA coal rules, the BLM RMPs and the BLM coal leasing ban. I think continuing to fund our AG to litigate these issues is critical. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

Local control should always be our first priority. If asked for assistance, we need to help but we should never assume that our local governments are not capable of handling many issues

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

I think we can offer assistance on rural health care as well as infrastructure assistance for development impact.

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

Collaberation is extremely necessary to bring forward solid common sense, conservative legislation.

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

Our wildlife is a treasure that should always be protected. I have worked many years in the energy industry and have helped develop mitigation plans to ensure habitat protection with responsible energy development. Real estate encroachment is a continuing issue. Some areas will definitely be off limits to any growth.
