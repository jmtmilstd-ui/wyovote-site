T. Deb Wolfley

T. Deb Wolfley (Republican, Fairview): Former Lincoln County sheriff (9 years) and Lincoln County commissioner (8 years); executive director of the Lincoln County School District #2 Education Foundation (self-described, 2024 iVoterGuide biography); Lincoln County, Wyoming, Sheriff; Lincoln County, Wyoming, Commissioner. Lost the Aug. 20, ate House District 21 with 23.6% of the vote.

Republican · Fairview, Lincoln County

Primary result: Lost, 559 votes (23.6%), 2nd of 2.

Research

OccupationFormer Lincoln County sheriff (9 years) and Lincoln County commissioner (8 years); executive director of the Lincoln County School District #2 Education Foundation (self-described, 2024 iVoterGuide biography)
Background

I have lived in my district, HD # 21 pretty much all my life. I have a wonderful wife, 6 children and 15 grandchildren. I have been involved in government work many years in this area. Served on our local school board for 7 years, served at Lincoln County Sheriff for 9 years and deputy for 7 years. I served as town administrator the Town of Alpine for 3 years and served for 8 years of Lincoln County Commissioner. I have a 4yr degree in Police Science and a Masters of Public Administration. I worked for a midsize corporation for a few years and my wife and I owned and operated a retail store in Afton for 20 years. …

On his 2024 iVoterGuide biography he listed a B.A. in police science from Weber State College (1975), an M.P.A. from Brigham Young University (1988), nine years as Lincoln County sheriff, eight years as a Lincoln County commissioner, and affiliations including membership in the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, executive director of the Lincoln County School District #2 Education Foundation, Boy Scout leader, and Star Valley Arts Council member.

Positions

How, within the framework of a lawmaker's powers and duties, would you address the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today?

The challenges and opportunities that Wyoming is currently facing and will face in the future needs to rely on the constitution of our great state. Legislators are elected to represent the citizens of their district and the state. …

As a state lawmaker, what will guide your approach to local control? How do you determine when a decision should be left to local leaders or made by the Wyoming Legislature?

Government at its best is one where people in their communities govern themselves as best as they can. They know what is important to them and should work together to achieve their goals and plans. Cities and towns establish their ordinances to provide basic laws to govern their citizens. …

Wyoming has wrestled for years with issues of housing, health care, education and economic development. What do you believe the state's role should be in supporting the well-being of Wyoming citizens and their families?

The well-being or our Wyoming citizens and families has been and will always be on the fore-front of discussion. It boils down to the guding principles of the constitution in provding services to our citizens. We cannot be everything to everyone. Education is vitally important to everyone in our state. …

How willing are you to compromise with legislators and other officials with different perspectives?

Government has a long history of compromise between legislators and other officials. It is not a perfect world, thus in order to get things accomplished, there generally has to be some compromise. I certainly am aware of this need to get along and work with various government officials. …

How do you balance the often competing demands of wildlife and open spaces versus energy, industrial and real estate development?

Both areas are highly important to the legacy of Wyoming. Is it possible to appease both sides on this issue? I believe it is possible. It will take some strong and clear thinking to come up with solutions to this never ending matter. …
