Sheryl Foland

Sheryl Foland (Republican, Rawlins): Owner and clinical therapist at Course Corrections LLC in Rawlins; licensed clinical social worker in Wyoming. A professional résumé posted by the Wyoming Employee Assistance Program describes therapy, consulting, and work as a mental-health evaluator and expert witness for the state under the Title 25 emergency-detention process. Lost the Aug. 20, 2024 Republican primary for State House District 15 with 16.8% of the vote.

Republican · Rawlins, Carbon County

Primary result: Lost, 196 votes (16.8%), 3rd of 3.

Background

Rawlins licensed clinical social worker and owner of Course Corrections LLC (since February 2017, per her professional résumé). The résumé also lists prior clinical work in Casper and Evanston, adjunct teaching at Eastern New Mexico University–Roswell, and earlier criminal-justice and treatment-program work in Texas, New Mexico, and Kansas. On her 2024 iVoterGuide biography she listed affiliations including past Rotary president, St. Joseph’s Catholic Church pastoral council, Carbon County Republican Party secretary, RNC convention delegate, and legislative-advocate roles with addiction-professional groups. She was 2018/2020 secretary of the NASW Wyoming chapter, per the résumé. Street address, phone, and email on the résumé were not stored.

EducationMaster of Social Work, New Mexico Highlands University (iVoterGuide lists 2012; her résumé also lists the M.S.W. from NMHU). Bachelor of Arts in psychology and Bachelor of Science in criminal justice, Wichita State University, 2003. Associate degree, Butler County Community College, 2002 (iVoterGuide; her résumé specifies an Associate of Science in police science and addiction studies). Her résumé also lists a Master of Arts in liberal studies candidacy at Wichita State and a certificate in forensic criminology.
Positions

Abortion, economy and health (2024 voter-guide questionnaire)

On her iVoterGuide form she wrote that any abortion decision “should be made by the mother, her family and healthcare professionals,” strongly agreed that abortion providers should not receive taxpayer funds, and disagreed with legalizing assisted suicide. She strongly agreed that free enterprise and private property are essential, said employers should determine wages, and opposed using ESG ratings when investing public money.
