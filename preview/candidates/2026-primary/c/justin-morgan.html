Justin Morgan

Justin Morgan (Republican, Riverton): Runs a horse-training business in Riverton; U.S. Marine Corps veteran. Lost the Nov. 3, 2026 general election for State Representative H33 with 34.9% of the vote.

House District 33 · Republican

General result: State House HD33, Republican, 741 votes (34.9%), Lost.

Research

ResidenceRiverton, Fremont County
OccupationRuns a horse-training business in Riverton
Background

Riverton resident (mailing city on the candidate filing). Filed Aug. 27, 2026, as the Republican nominee for House District 33, after the August primary; he did not appear in the 2026 primary candidate store.

Nominated as a Republican write-in in the Aug. 18, 2026 primary for House District 33 (41 votes, 35.3% of the named write-in total reported on Ballotpedia; there was no named Republican on the primary ballot). Lost the Nov. 3, 2026 general election to incumbent Ivan Posey.

Wyoming native and Marine Corps veteran who ran businesses in North Carolina before moving back in 2004. Has lived in Riverton about 20 years, where he runs a horse-training business. Told a League of Women Voters forum he had not thought about politics until people approached him, and that his children are homeschooled through a program with Riverton High School. His mother was a teacher her whole life. He has two daughters.

MilitaryU.S. Marine Corps veteran
Issue positions

Property taxes

Supports Proposition 1, the 50% property-tax cut for longtime owners. He said property taxes have gone up 73% since 2019 by his count, and that his research showed the cut would amount to 1.8% of the budget, which governments should be able to adjust.

Medicaid

Opposes Medicaid expansion, saying it would raise taxes on people who are already struggling and that hospitals will see anyone who walks in.

Schools

Said last session’s school-funding recalibration needs work and that school-board members he spoke with wanted money earmarked for teacher raises. Supports vouchers for homeschool families but said students on state money should take the same tests as public-school students. Supports full funding for the basket of goods, including art, music, and science.

Public lands and energy

Opposes selling public land (“I don’t think we should ever sell one square foot of public land”) but would like the state to have more control over it. Supports making corner crossing legal. Not a big supporter of AI data centers; wants companies to pay into an escrow account for cleanup, be taxed at a higher rate, and have no access to water without water rights. Said wind and solar clutter the landscape and kill wildlife, though he could support rooftop solar. On state revenue he said, “Drill baby drill.”

Elections

Supports requiring a state ID to vote. Supports secure ballot drop boxes and opposes purging voters from the rolls for skipping a general election. Opposes open primaries and supports replacing voting machines with hand counts.

Public safety and hunting

Said in his closing that he would never vote to defund police, fire, or ambulance services or take away hunting and fishing.
