Andrew Server

Andrew Server (Republican, Cheyenne): High school teacher. Won the Aug. 18, 2026 Republican primary for State House District 61 with 57.6% of the vote.

House District 61 · Republican

Primary result: Won

Research

ResidenceCheyenne, Laramie County
OccupationHigh school teacher
Background

Andrew Server graduated from Chaparral High School. He earned a bachelor's degree and additional certification from the University of Wyoming in 2016 and 2018, respectively. His career experience includes working as a high school teacher.

I am a husband, father of three wonderful kids, a teacher, and a strong conservative. I have been a teacher for eight years and know firsthand many of the issues in our public education system. My initial degree was in political science and I have experience working as a legislative aide in the Colorado capitol. I will bring my experience as a teacher, father, and dedicated resident to the position of representative.

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

I am optimistic for Wyoming. With education, the legislature got a lot right in its recent recalibration. There are still many issues facing education and I will bring the unique perspective of an educator to the discussion. To provide a robust public option, curriculum needs to be efficacious and knowledge based. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Transparency for medical bills should be continued and further emboldened. The Hospital Price Transparency Act was a step in the correct direction with that regard, but SF 57 took its teeth out. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

Nearly half of Wyoming is under federal control. I support Wyoming lands being in Wyoming hands. That doesn't mean the full privatization of those lands, but I believe that interests and stakes are greater for those with a closer connection to the land than a bureaucrat in Washington. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

In my conversations with hopeful constituents, nearly all have expressed appreciation for the reduction of property taxes. Several also have brought up that property taxes feel arbitrary with home values and thus property taxes increasing often outside of their control. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

Saying that the government ought to play an active role in facilitating economic sounds nice and, in some circumstances, it might be done altruistically in practice. However, it can seem as if publicly funded institutions that seek to play an active role in economic development end up choosing winners and losers. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

I consider campaign donations as an extension or form of the First Amendment. A private individual or association of individuals in the form of PACs can choose to use their money to indicate whose voice/what issue they support in the public sphere. …
