Karen Weber

Karen Weber (Republican, Yoder): Registered nurse with a B.S.N. and M.B.A.; decades of experience in business development and operations, including writing business plans and healthcare policy and procedure (self-described in a 2026 Torrington Telegram candidate questionnaire). Lost the Aug. 18, 2026 Republican primary for State House District 5 with 23.7% of the vote.

Background

Lost the Aug. 18, 2026 Republican primary for House District 5 to Joe Speckner, 614 (23.8%) to 1,969 (76.2%). The Torrington Telegram published candidate answers, not read in full here.

In the same questionnaire she said she had transferable financial and business skills developed through her nursing and M.B.A. education and “decades of experience (also known as the school of hard knocks) in business development and operations all over the country,” and that she had gone door to door since May listening to constituents, whose most numerous concerns were that it had “become too hard” to live financially and that youth continue to leave.

EducationR.N., B.S.N., and M.B.A. (self-described in a 2026 Torrington Telegram candidate questionnaire; institutions not named in that questionnaire)
Issue positions

Property taxes and the local economy

In the Telegram questionnaire she proposed creating a “Citizens Wealth Growth Fund” to pay down residential property taxes and eventually eliminate them, and argued for “nominal value” assessments rather than fair-market values that change with the market, calling a 48% increase from 2019–2024 unsustainable. At an August 2026 forum she focused opening remarks on the local economy, saying seniors were being squeezed by inflation while Social Security stayed flat, and that growing new local business was central to her platform.

Education, health care and priorities

She said targeted education funding should bring resources closest to the student and that teachers need good wages. She listed priorities she had heard from constituents: economy and economic freedom, agriculture and food production, “life/best health” including healthcare access and affordability, liberty and freedom, and the Republican platform, and wrote that as an “RN BSN MBA” she would be “a strong voice to assist in resolving Wyoming’s health care crisis.”
