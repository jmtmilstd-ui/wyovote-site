Britney Tennant

Britney Tennant (Democratic, Cheyenne): Nonprofit executive director. Unopposed in the Aug. 18, 2026 Democratic primary for State House District 11.

House District 11 · Democratic

Primary result: Unopposed

Background

Britney Tennant graduated from Cheyenne Central High School. She earned a bachelor's degree from the University of Wyoming in 2004 and a graduate degree from Regis University in 2017. Her career experience includes working as a nonprofit executive director.

I am a lifelong Wyomingite, born in Wheatland and raised in Cheyenne. My career is defined by executive leadership and community advocacy. As CEO of the Cheyenne Animal Shelter and founder of Black Dog Animal Rescue, I have managed large-scale operations, balanced complex budgets, and built statewide coalitions. I hold a Master’s in Nonprofit Management (MNM) and am a Certified Animal Welfare Administrator (CAWA). My civic dedication runs deep. Appointed by Governor Gordon to the Wyoming State Board of Veterinary Medicine, I have worked directly on statewide regulatory oversight. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Wyoming’s greatest challenge and opportunity lie in securing a vibrant future for our young people and creating communities where every family can thrive. True long-term fiscal stability doesn't just come from balancing budgets; it comes from investing in our people. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

To ensure healthcare stability, the Legislature must prioritize a pro-people framework that leverages every available funding source. I support utilizing the proposed healthcare perpetuity fund to secure predictable, long-term investments in our medical infrastructure. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

I do not support the sale or transfer of public lands. Our public lands are Wyoming’s greatest legacy, and responsibly managing them is critical to protecting our wildlife, preserving our heritage, and maintaining our thriving outdoor recreation and tourism industries. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

I do not support further property tax reductions. While relief for struggling homeowners is important, we must recognize that Wyoming taxes directly fund the essential public services our communities rely on. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The Legislature’s role in economic development must be pro-people and rooted in meaningful collaboration. State entities like the Wyoming Business Council should serve as supportive partners, working hand-in-hand with our municipalities rather than imposing top-down mandates. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

Wyoming’s system for monitoring campaign finance requires meaningful reform to ensure absolute transparency and protect the integrity of our elections. My chief concern centers on the rise of independent expenditures by third-party organizations. …
