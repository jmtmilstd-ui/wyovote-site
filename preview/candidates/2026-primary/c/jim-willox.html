Jim Willox

Jim Willox (Republican, Douglas): Converse County Commissioner (19.5 years); Converse County Commissioner. Lost the Aug. 18, 2026 Republican primary for State House District 6 with 47.6% of the vote.

Background

Jim Willox earned a high school diploma from Douglas High School and a bachelor's degree from the University of Wyoming in 1989.

I was raised on a ranch south of Douglas but moved to town 25 years ago. I am a Douglas High School graduate and have a BS in Agriculture Economics from the University of Wyoming. I have served as a County Commissioner for the past 19.5 years and that has provided me with a wealth of knowledge and experience. I have advocated for Converse County and Wyoming at the state and national level, including the US Departments of Energy, Interior, Agriculture, BLM and EPA. Multiple use, streamlined regulations and local control are a regular theme when I am speaking to those agencies. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

We need to continue investing in economic development and diversity by appropriately funding and directing the Business council so they can be an effective partner with local entities as they seek out and grow local businesses. If we fail to stay in the game as some have suggested, we lose, plain and simple. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

First of all, we need to make sure health care is available. Rural hospitals are in desperate shape and lack of care is real. The health care transformation proposal from the Dept of Health was trying to help ensure this in the long term. Unfortunately, the proposal was rejected by the feds. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

* There should not be any large-scale sale or trade of public lands. Public lands in Wyoming are a treasurer and we must protect them. They provide recreation, wildlife habitat, support agriculture, mineral wealth and a host of other benefits. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

Targeted property tax relief has been very effective in Wyoming. As a County Commissioner I advocated for and voted to implement the highly successful Converse County Property Tax refund program. Over the last 3 years we have refunded over $620,000 to residential owners in Converse County. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

*As noted before, we must remain in the economic development game. If we fail to play, we lose. Some have characterized economic development as picking winners and losers, that is incredibly short-sighted. It is about creating jobs for our kids and grandkids. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

I believe the campaign reporting requirements for candidates is clear and transparent, which is very important. Filing donation reports a week before the primary allows the voters to know who is supporting each candidate and evaluate accordingly.
