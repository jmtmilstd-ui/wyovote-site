Background

Christopher Knapp's career experience includes owning a business.

I am a fourth-generation resident of Wyoming. I own several small businesses and have to children both grown. I have 12 years of experience as a Campbell County Commissioner in my younger days and 5 years of experience as a Wyoming State House member. I am Chairman of Corporations, Elections and Political Subdivisions, Chairman of Management Audit and sit as a member of the Minerals Business and Economic Development Committee. I have also served on Travel Recreation and Wildlife as well as the Select Committee on Capital Finance and Investing. My years as a commissioner allow me to weigh local government needs and partnerships while giving me budget experience. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Wyoming continues to face challenges for future revenues which have typically come from our minerals industry/ Those revenues due to past "climate" administrations have been on the decline. With our current national need for energy dominance Wyoming should reduce restrictive regulations for our legacy industries. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Healthcare is an issue for most rural communities. Cost is a result of patient expectation, increased technology and expense to meet the expectation and the insurance industry as well as Medicaid and Medicare that pay for that cost ultimately increasing for the patient. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

The federal government should transfer land and minerals to the state in an effort to equalize the eastern states entry into statehood. The west is at a disadvantage in developing both minerals and land use. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

When we passed property tax relief it was the height of inflation and costs of insurance, medications, food and energy. Folks were deciding if they had to skip eating or paying property tax. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

Government should use regulation reduction, tax relief when appropriate and building up infrastructure such as roads, water and sewer, and other infrastructure helping communities to prepare for growth. We should not be picking individual companies for cash infusion. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

Campaign donations are protected by the Supreme court ruling and the free-speech rights of the constitution. It would be great to get money out of politics but challenging facing the use of donations as a voice to represent our views and votes as citizens. …
