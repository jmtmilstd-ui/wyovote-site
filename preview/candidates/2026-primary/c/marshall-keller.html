Background

Marshall Keller served in the U.S. Navy from 1997 to 2025.

I am a Navy veteran, engineer, attorney, small rancher, small business owner, and member of the Hot Springs County School Board. I served as a Navy SEAL with SEAL Team Three and later returned to the Navy Reserves, retiring in 2025 after additional deployments. After earning a degree in Geological Engineering from Montana Tech, I worked in mining and engineering throughout the West, including Wyoming. I later earned my law degree and currently practice law, representing local municipalities. My wife, Natalya, and I live in Thermopolis, where our two daughters attended school. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

I do not support the wholesale sale or transfer of public lands. Wyoming's public lands are a vital part of our heritage and provide opportunities for hunting, fishing, recreation, grazing, mineral development, and tourism. These public lands are a part of Wyoming culture and belong to the people. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

Families across Wyoming are feeling the effects of rising costs, and the government should be just as mindful of spending taxpayer dollars. The Legislature has already provided significant property tax relief through the recent 25% residential property tax reduction and other existing exemptions. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The Legislature has an important role in economic development. The government cannot create prosperity, but it can support the infrastructure, workforce development, and business climate that allows communities to succeed. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?
