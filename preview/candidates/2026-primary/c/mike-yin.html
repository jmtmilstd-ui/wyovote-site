Background

Mike Yin earned a bachelor's degree in computer science from Carnegie Mellon University in 2007. Yin’s career experience includes working as a senior software developer with Android.

I am a software engineer and small business owner, and I have had the honor of representing the most of the Town of Jackson in the Wyoming House since 2019, currently serving as House Minority Leader. I earned a computer science degree from Carnegie Mellon and spent a decade writing software before running for office.

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Polarization has kept us from working on Wyoming. It has kept us from ensuring communities can thrive. It has threatened our access to the outdoors. And it has brought the vitriol from DC to Wyoming. As Minority Leader I work across the aisle, because supporting Wyoming's communities has never been a partisan project.

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

The single most effective thing we could do is still pass Medicaid Expansion. Even after HR1, it would still reduce much of our uncompensated care which essentially gets passed on to all other payers.

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

No.

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

The public passed "Amendment A" to allow us to tax primary homes differently from other properties. Instead of implementing the option the citizens of Wyoming gave us, we've made an endless confusing shotgun approach to relief. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

If we truly believe in thriving communities and ensuring our kids can grow up and start a business or find a great career, we will need to be part of making that happen.

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

Yes. The people should know who spends money on our elections.
