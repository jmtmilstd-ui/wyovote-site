Jessica Crowder

Jessica Crowder (Republican, Cheyenne): Rancher and agriculture/conservation policy consultant; former executive director, Wyoming Stock Growers Land Trust; former policy advisor to Gov. Matt Mead. Won the Aug. 18, 2026 Republican primary for State House District 43 with 62.9% of the vote.

House District 43 · Republican

Primary result: Won

General result: State House HD43, Republican, 2,443 votes (100%), Unopposed.

Background

I grew up on a cattle ranch, an experience that led me to Texas Tech University, where I earned a degrees in Range Science, focusing my graduate research on prescribed fire and grazing. Today, my husband, Jason, and I own and operate our ranch in Laramie County and a consulting company. Through consulting, I provide expertise in agriculture, conservation, and policy. Before focusing on our businesses, I served as Executive Director of the Wyoming Stock Growers Land Trust and as a policy advisor to former Wyoming Governor Matt Mead. I’ve also worked with the Western Landowners Alliance, the WY Department of Agriculture, the Agricultural Research Service, and Northwest College. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Wyoming is built on hard work, strong communities, and the responsible use of our natural resources. We face real challenges — from pressures on our core industries to the need for a more resilient, diverse economy and the increasing outmigration of our young people. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

The rising cost of healthcare and the difficulty of accessing services are real challenges for Wyoming families, especially in rural communities. We need reforms that reduce costs, expand access, and meet people where they are. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

I do not support broad efforts to sell or transfer Wyoming’s public lands. Our federal lands provide real value to the people of this state — from recreation and hunting to livestock grazing and mineral development. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

Property taxes must be continually refined so people can afford to buy homes, stay in their homes, and build equity and pride of place. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The Legislature plays an important role in strengthening economic development. At the forefront of that role is creating clear, consistent processes and regulations so businesses have confidence when they look to Wyoming. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

I appreciate every campaign donation because it represents trust in the work I’m doing and the future we’re building for Wyoming. When someone chooses to give, they’re partnering in a vision—not trying to influence it. I take that seriously. …
