Background

Rothfuss obtained a B.A. in International Studies, an M.S. in Chemical Engineering from the University of Wyoming, and an M.S. in Applied Physics and a Ph.D. in Chemical Engineering from the University of Washington. His career experience includes working as a chemical engineer and consultant.

I grew up in Casper, graduated from Natrona County High School, and attended the University of Wyoming as a Trustees’ Scholar. At UW, I received a BA in International Studies, captained the Cowboy Debate Team, and completed an MS in Chemical Engineering. I earned a PhD in Chemical Engineering and an MS in Applied Physics from the University of Washington. Heather and I returned to Laramie in 2006 to raise our four kids. I am a faculty member in the UW Honors College where I have taught since 2007. My career has crossed engineering, advanced and emerging technologies, diplomacy, higher education and public policy, including service as an AAAS S&T Diplomacy Fellow at the U.S. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Wyoming’s biggest challenge is that our economy and tax structure too often push away the people we most need to keep, including our young people, skilled workers, entrepreneurs and families building their futures. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Wyoming is failing on healthcare, and we need to admit it and change. First, Wyoming must expand Medicaid. We are one of only 10 states that still have not, leaving roughly 19,000 Wyomingites without coverage while rejecting well over $1 billion in federal support that could strengthen families, providers and rural …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

I strongly oppose selling, transferring or privatizing public lands in Wyoming. These lands belong to the people, not billionaires, private interests or politicians looking for budget fixes. Some leaders, including in Wyoming, continue looking for ways to sell off or transfer our public lands. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

Property taxes have risen because assessed valuations have increased dramatically, creating pressure for many Wyoming homeowners. But property taxes are essential. They fund K-12 education, community colleges, counties, municipalities, special districts, hospitals and other important local services. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

I support a strong, positive role for the Legislature in economic development, but that role should not be picking winners and losers or simply writing checks to favored industries. Our job is to build the conditions where Wyoming people, communities, entrepreneurs, workers, and businesses can succeed. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

I have never taken a single dollar of PAC money. That is intentional. Wyoming’s campaign finance system, like the national system, is legalized and structured corruption. It favors wealthy donors, PACs and special interests over citizens, and then pretends no harm is done unless someone proves a quid pro quo. …
