Background

Ken Chestek was born in Cincinnati, Ohio. Chestek earned a bachelor's degree from Penn State University in 1975 and a law degree from the University of Pittsburgh in 1979. His career experience includes working as a professor.

I was a practicing lawyer in Pennsylvania for over 20 years when I decided to pursue a career in academia. I have taught in law schools in Michigan and Indiana before accepting a position at the University of Wyoming College of Law 14 years ago. I retired from that position two years ago and now hold the rank of Professor Emeritus. I specialized in teaching legal writing and trial advocacy. I served a two-year term as President of the Legal Writing Institute (an organization of over 2000 legal writing professors around the country and into Canada). I have co-authored a groundbreaking book on legal writing (now in its third edition). …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

1. Affordable housing 2. Job opportunities These two issues are the main drivers of outmigration of our young people. We need to help facilitate new industries and new, good-paying jobs to attract young workers, while also doing what we can to drive down the cost of housing. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Expand Medicaid. Support rural hospitals and health care delivery, including EMS services. Find ways to address the maternity deserts that are cropping up all over the state. Continue or expand support for WWAMI so that more young doctors locate in and hopefully remain in the state.

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

Absolutely not. I will support all efforts to let our federal representatives know that sale of federal land is a terrible idea, even if that land is transferred to state ownership. At the state level, I will continue to resist any effort to sell state land to private interests. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

I have proposed to the Joint Revenue Committee a new way of assessing land. The model is called "base year assessments." After a countywide fair-market-value assessment, all tax assessments would be frozen at that "base year" value for some period of time (say, 5 years) before being re-balanced. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

At a minimum, the legislature should insure a level playing field so that all businesses, large and small, have equal access to resources and are not unfairly burdened by taxes or fees. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

I am in favor of "bright money" while I am steadfastly opposed to "dark money." I define "dark money" as money that cannot be traced back to any individual or group of individuals. Thus, most political action committees (PACs) would be considered bright money, since they must report their donors to the state. …
