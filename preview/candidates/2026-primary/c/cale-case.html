Background

Case earned his B.A. and Ph.D. in economics and currently works as an economist. He was a candidate for United States Representative, Wyoming, District At-Large in 2004.

I am an economist (PhD) with a long record of public service in the Wyoming Legislature. Except for the years I was working away or going to school, I have lived in Wyoming my whole life. I have had the great privilege of working around the world on public policy issues involving governance and democracy, natural resources and public utilities. With my son and his mom, I built and operate a successful business in Lander. I have made a payroll for 30 years. I am proud to be a thoughtful and effective legislative leader where I have served on the Revenue, Corporations (Corporations, Elections and Political Subdivisions, and Tribal Committees, frequently as Chairman. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

The most immediate threat to Wyoming has been this short-term turn to populism advanced by new proponents under the Republican banner. Newer members of the Legislature, elected in a populist wave, have produced conflicting and often unconstitutional policy directions for Wyoming. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Healthcare is important to everyone, but the working poor suffer the most. Expansion of health services for the working poor would make peoples' lives better and improve productivity overall while putting money back in our communities to support healthcare facilities for all. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

No. Our public lands are a treasure that we need to maintain for future generations. Limited exchanges are needed on occasion in very specific circumstances. Our Office of State Lands does a pretty good job of managing our state lands. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

The populist relief efforts have produced bad public policy and that has not made Wyoming better. Property taxes fund local governments and education - not state services. These shortsighted efforts have increased local government funding disparity. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

Most recently government's role came to light in a populist driven attack on the Wyoming Business Council. Most of our communities lack facilities and expertise necessary to attract business interests. If low taxes were the complete answer, Wyoming would be overwhelmed with new economic activity. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

I have never taken a PAC contribution for legislative races until this year. I made this decision reluctantly because the cost of races has skyrocketed and rational policy promoters are being outgunned by unaccountable third-party ideologues from in and out of state. …
