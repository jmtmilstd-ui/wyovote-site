Kenneth D Roberts

Kenneth D Roberts (Republican, Kemmerer): Clerk of District Court (elected; 32 years); Wyoming Game and Fish Commission member; Clerk of District Court (elected); Wyoming Game and Fish Commission (president 2022-2023). Won the Aug. 18, 2026 Republican primary for State House District 18 with 55.1% of the vote.

Background

I have served System as elected Clerk of the District Court, where I have worked to uphold the integrity of the Judicial system and ensured accountability and fairness under the law. Currently a member of the Wyoming Game and Fish Commission being president from 2022-2023. Prior served 8 years on the Wyoming Board of Parol, the last two being chairman. Served on the state committee for Lincoln Bicentennial.

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

The State's economy depends on oil, coal, and natural gas to generate significant tax revenue, but consistent long-term market changes and price swings make them too susceptible to outside influence that we cannot control. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

As a legislator I would champion Wyoming's heath care system. Recruit physicians, nurses, mental health providers. Keeping our local hospitals in business and clinics assessable is a must. Paying for the medical services in rural areas is an obligation and I believe a big part of living in our great state. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

I would never support any selling or transferring public land in Wyoming. Public land is Public. Everyone that lives in Wyoming enjoys outdoor recreation and our magnificent wildlife. We are entrusted to protect and preserve or public land for future generations and wildlife.

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

In Wyoming, property taxes do not mainly fund the state government, they fund local communities. They pay for county roads, sheriff's offices fire districts, libraries, emergency services, senior centers, weed and pest districts, and many of the everyday services people rely on without even thinking about them. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The legislature should help economic development not hinder it. Let the Business council, Wyoming Business Alliance, city chambers and other economic development organizations lead the charge, they are the professionals who know what is best for the state. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

I see no problem at this time as long as we maintain full transparency and we monitor it closely, if needed we can create new legislation.
