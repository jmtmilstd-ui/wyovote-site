Background

Trey Sherwood earned a degree in history and English from Piedmont College in 2001 and a master's degree in public history from the University of West Georgia in 2004. Sherwood's career experience includes working as the director of Laramie Main Street and the administrating director of the Laramie Mural Project.

I have 20 years of community building experience in Laramie, as an economic developer, a non profit director and public historian. I am a graduate of Leadership Wyoming, co-founder of the Albany County Housing Coalition, Laramie Mural Project, and am the senior member of the House Appropriations Committee. I utilize civility, honesty, optimism and empathy as essential leadership skills to serve my constituents, small businesses and hard working families. I enjoy learning new things and building relationships based on shared values. I am passionate about attainable housing, opportunities for young people, public education, access to mental health services and recreating on public lands.

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

We need to address the outmigration of our youth if we are to build strong communities. Young people want good paying jobs, attainable housing, affordable healthcare, access to mental health and maternity services, vibrant downtowns and outdoor amenities. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Wyoming is one of the most expensive states in the nation for health care. To address this, I will continue to support Medicaid expansion, increases to reimbursement rates for maternal and rural EMS services, educational incentive training and recruitment programs. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

Public lands belong to the public. They are NOT to be traded for corporate gain or sold to the highest bidder for private use. We need to ensure our state agencies that provide recreation (State Parks), hunting opportunities (Game & Fish), and conservation (Forest Service, WWNRT) are staffed appropriately with budgets …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

Property tax relief should be targeted to those who need it most. I do not support blanket reductions for those able to pay, i.e. second homeowners and the ultra rich. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The state should facilitate a landscape where hard working Wyomingites can innovate. I support policies that increase economic opportunities, good paying jobs and investments in community infrastructure. …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

Constituents should have full access to the donations accepted by or spent on behalf of a candidate's campaign. From individual gifts to out-of-state corporations, voters have the right to know who is investing in and influencing our political process. …
