Background

Karlee Provenza was born in Pueblo, Colorado. Provenza earned a B.A. in psychology from the University of Northern Colorado in 2013, an M.A. in experimental psychology from the University of Wyoming in 2016, and a Ph.D. from the University of Wyoming in 2020. Her career experience includes working as an educator, waitress, private investigator, and professional photographer.

It is an honor to currently be serving my third term. I serve on the Agriculture and Public Lands Committee and the Travel, Recreation, and Wildlife Committee. I have previously served on the Education and the Judiciary Committees. During my time in the legislature, I have passed laws protecting public land access and various bills aimed at government transparency and accountability. In 2024, after years of community organizing, I helped pass legislation that strengthened oversight of law enforcement in Wyoming. I am a nonprofit coach for organizations aiming to build grassroots power. …

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

The greatest issues facing Wyoming is healthcare, the heartbreak of our young people leaving due to lack of opportunities, and out of state extremists funneling resources into Wyoming to divide us and dismantle our public institutions. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Given the recent cuts to healthcare at the federal level, the state should work towards providing insurance and healthcare services to the people of Wyoming beyond Medicaid. …

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

Hell no, I do not support efforts to sell off our public land. The idea that the federal government could transfer lands to the state for management is just a foot-in-the-door technique towards public land sales. The state does not have the resources to manage these lands and would ultimately sell most of them off. …

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

The legislature has created a shell game where we have no plan for how to make up for lost revenues to pay for services, so some day the people of Wyoming will be forced to pay through increased sales tax or other means. No plan is a bad plan. …

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

If the free market and low taxes were going to solve our economic diversification problem, it would have done so already. The state needs to help solve some of the problems that keep businesses from coming here – namely our housing shortage, unaffordable and inaccessible healthcare, and opportunities for young …

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

I believe that money in politics is corrupting our entire system. I don’t take AIPAC money and I never will. I prefer our campaign is funded by small donations from working class people who already share my values. …
