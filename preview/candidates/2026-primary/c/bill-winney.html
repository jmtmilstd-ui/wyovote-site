Bill Winney

Background

Bill Winney was born in Bondurant, Wyoming. He served in the U.S. Navy from 1968 to 2002. He earned a high school diploma from Wakefield High School. Winney earned a bachelor's degree from the U.S. Naval Academy in 1972 and a master's degree from the Catholic University of America in 2000. His career experience includes working as a nuclear engineer.

30 years active duty, US Navy. Nuclear Submarine Force. BS US Naval Academy, MSE The Catholic University of America. Eagle Scout. Active in observing and speaking to our legislature.

Issue positions

What are the biggest challenges and opportunities facing Wyoming today? As a legislator, how would you address them?

Water in the Colorado watershed. Energy development to include nuclear. AI and data centers. Regarding the watershed, there will come a time when it is in court. Wyoming must be prepared for this. Continued development of coal, oil and natural gas is important to our economy and tax receipts. …

We asked WyoFile readers to rank issues that are important to them, and healthcare costs and access topped the list. What can the Legislature do to make healthcare more affordable and accessible to Wyomingites?

Increased staffing. The Program that pays education costs in exchange for future practice in Wyoming is very good and must be supported. Better use of internet and tele-medicine is important. Facility support should be considered.

Do you support efforts to sell or transfer public land in Wyoming? How do you plan to ensure that state and federal land is managed in the best interest of the people of Wyoming?

Public access to public lands must be maintained (eg hunting, fishing and recreation). I find roadless areas too extensive for good forest management.

No two Wyoming counties are the same when it comes to how they generate revenue. As a legislator, how would you approach the public's desire for property tax relief with local revenue needs?

There are several ways to support these elements of county services. Probably the simplest is sales tax. As electric power generation expands taxation there may be useful. Property tax relief should be carefully applied to residential properties.

What role, if any, should the Wyoming Legislature play in economic development?

The Legislature must ensure that bureaucracy rules are minimized. Bureaucracies always tend to expand their reach and authority, the legislature must pay attention to that. Bureaucracies can expand to fill the money available. Too many "good ideas" can spoil an economy.

Explain your values when it comes to campaign donations. Does Wyoming's system for monitoring campaign donations and expenditures need reform?

Reform is not needed. The people should be able to donate to their candidate of choice. The only thing that needs to be carefully monitored is out of state donations.
